### The core buyer insight V1 encodes

> The target customer is **better at their job than their online presence suggests.**
> The pain is not "I need a website" — it is *"I am being judged as smaller and less competent
> than I am."*

Stated examples in V1: a restoration contractor with twenty years of craft and a Wix site; an
academic coach with a real practice and no website at all.

### Buyer situations named in V1 service page H2s

- Web design → *"Businesses whose website has to earn its cost."*
- Website redesign → *"Sites that still work, and no longer represent you."*
- Local SEO → *"Businesses whose customers are nearby and searching now."*
- Google Business Profile → *"Any business that gets found on Maps before it gets found on Google."*
- Brand strategy → *"Businesses that are better than they look."*

### Buying behaviour observed in a real engagement

From the Rare Raleigh Restoration case study — **restoration/urgent home services specifically**:

- Urgent, local, high-trust purchase
- Search happens **on a phone**
- Buyer compares **a small number of options quickly**
- Buyer calls **whoever looks most capable of handling the situation**

⚠️ This is documented for restoration contracting. It is **not** established for academic coaching,
pool leak detection, or any other vertical.

### What the one existing testimonial reveals about buyer priorities

Missy Boyd's review emphasises, in her own order: collaboration and being asked for input;
responsiveness (never more than a couple of hours, any day of the week); professionalism and
knowledge; **speed — two weeks start to finish**; the outcome covering both website *and*
branding/logo.

⚠️ One review. Treat as a signal, not a pattern.

## TO RESEARCH — Step 2

Do not fill any of these in from assumption. Each requires actual evidence.

### Buyer identity
- Who actually makes the buying decision, and who else is in the room
- Business size, revenue band, employee count of a typical Kreated buyer
- Whether the real buyer is local-Raleigh or geography-agnostic

### Trigger events
- What specifically happens right before someone contacts an agency
- Whether the trigger is embarrassment, a lost deal, a competitor, a rebrand, growth, or a failure

### Prior attempts
- What they tried before (DIY builder, cheap freelancer, previous agency) and why it failed
- What they now distrust because of it

### Objections and fears
- Price sensitivity and what a "too expensive" reaction is anchored to
- Fear of losing existing SEO/traffic in a redesign
- Fear of a solo operator (bandwidth, bus factor, continuity)
- Timeline expectations

### Evaluation behaviour
- What they actually look at on an agency site, and in what order
- What makes them leave
- How many agencies they shortlist
- What proof format they trust (case study, screenshot, review, referral, portfolio)

### Language
- The words buyers use for their own problem — not the words agencies use
- Which of Kreated's load-bearing terms (`system`, `evidence`, `foundations`, `visibility`) land and
  which read as jargon

### Competitive set
- Which agencies a Raleigh buyer is actually comparing Kreated against
- What those competitors promise, and where they are vague

### Channel
- How a buyer arrives: search, referral, Google Business Profile, social, cold outreach
- ❓ V1 records no analytics data at all, so **no behavioural data exists.** Step 2 cannot rely on
  site analytics for this.

### Sources Step 2 may draw on
- Skyler's direct recall of real sales conversations (highest value, zero cost)
- The three real client engagements
- Public reviews and forum language in each vertical
- Competitor sites in the Raleigh market
