## Why this document exists rather than a rate card on the page

Two things are true at once, and they pull in opposite directions.

A buyer comparing three studios wants to know what an extra location page costs
before the call. Publishing that is a genuine advantage, and it is the same
argument that put the four engagement prices on the page in the first place.

But an add-on price published without its conditions becomes the ceiling the
buyer expects and the floor you have to defend. "Location pages $400" invites
"I'll take twelve" — which is exactly the mass-produced city-page work
`/pricing/` now explicitly says it does not do. The engagement prices survive
publication because each one is attached to a paragraph explaining who it is
for and what it excludes. An add-on line item has nowhere to put that.

**Recommendation: approve the figures below as internal quoting guidance, and
keep the page's "scoped per project" wording.** If any of them should go public
later, they need the same treatment the engagements got: a condition, an
exclusion, and a reason.

## Market evidence gathered

| What | What the published 2026 data says |
|---|---|
| Per-page pricing generally | No standard per-page rate exists in the market. Pricing tracks scope and team, not page count. Clutch's 2026 data across 79,000+ agency profiles puts most US rates at **$100–$149/hour**, most projects under $10,000. |
| Small-agency project range | Agencies of 2–5 staff: **$5,000–$25,000** for a small-business site. |
| Google Business Profile, one-time | **$300–$1,500** for a full audit and optimization; most setup fees cluster **$300–$500**. |
| Landing page, one-off | Freelance **$500–$3,000**. Boutique agency with copy included **$1,500–$3,000**. Productised one-offs **$1,499–$2,499**. |
| GA4 / Tag Manager | The tools are free. The cost is entirely configuration labour, so it prices as hours, not as a product. |

## Proposed figures

Each is expressed as a **from**, matching how every other number on the site
behaves. Ranges are deliberately wide at the top because the variables named in
"What moves the number" on `/pricing/` are real.

### Pages

| Add-on | Proposed | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Additional standard page** (matches existing templates, you supply the content) | **from $250** | This is layout plus placement, not research. Below $250 it is not worth writing a scope for; the admin costs more than the work. |
| **Additional service page** (researched, written, internally linked) | **from $450** | It carries keyword mapping and original copy, which is most of a Growth page's cost. Pricing it near the standard page would quietly undercut Growth, which is where service pages are supposed to be bought. |
| **Additional location page** | **from $550** | Deliberately the most expensive per-page item, and that is the point. It has to be justified by demand and written to stand alone or it is the mass-produced city page the site says it will not build. Pricing it *low* creates demand for the exact work you refuse. |

⚠ **The location-page figure is a policy decision, not a costing exercise.** If
it is set cheaply, the sales conversation becomes about volume. Recommend
holding it high and keeping the "only where demand supports it" condition
attached to every quote.

### Local search and profile work

| Add-on | Proposed | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Google Business Profile, one-time setup and optimization** (standalone, not part of an engagement) | **from $450** | Sits in the upper half of the observed $300–$500 setup band. Below that it competes with the $99 automated services, which is not the comparison this business wants. |
| **Profile recovery** (suspended, duplicated, or claimed by someone else) | **from $650** | Genuinely different work with an uncertain outcome, and it should never be quoted at the same number as a clean setup. |

### Measurement

| Add-on | Proposed | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Conversion tracking setup on an existing site** (GA4, Tag Manager, events for forms and calls) | **from $400** | Roughly three to four hours at the market rate. The software is free, so the price has to be defended as labour, which is why it should be quoted as a fixed scope rather than hourly. |
| **Call tracking configuration** | **from $350** plus the vendor's own subscription, billed to you directly | 🚫 Do not absorb a third-party subscription into a Kreated price. It makes the margin invisible and the renewal awkward. |

### Conversion

| Add-on | Proposed | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Single landing page on an existing site**, with copy | **from $850** | Below the $1,500 boutique-agency floor and well above the freelance floor, which is the correct position for a founder-led studio. |
| **Landing page without copy** (you supply it) | **from $550** | |

### Photography

| Add-on | Proposed | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Photography coordination** | **from $350**, photographer billed to you at cost | Coordination is a real service: brief, shot list, scheduling, selection, retouching direction. Marking up a photographer's invoice is not, and it creates a conflict about who the photographer works for. Recommend never marking it up, and saying so. |

### Advertising

**Recommend no published figure, and no add-on line at all.**

Google Ads is not an add-on to a website engagement, it is a different business
with a different risk profile, a monthly commitment and a media budget that
dwarfs the fee. Every other item on this page is bounded work with an end. If
ads are offered, they need their own page and their own conditions.

### Branding

**Already priced. Do not re-derive.**

The internal reference (*KREATED — Pricing, Offers, Scope & Client Onboarding
Reference*, §52–56) already carries **Brand Refresh $750+**, **Brand Identity
$1,500+**, and **Brand + Website $3,950–$5,950+**. `/pricing/` correctly shows
branding as scoped alongside an engagement rather than folded into one. Nothing
here should replace those figures.

## What I recommend publishing, if anything

If you want one add-on number on the page, make it the **additional standard
page**. It is the question buyers actually ask, it is the least dangerous to
publish, and it makes the three-to-five and six-to-ten page ceilings feel like
choices rather than limits.

Everything else earns more by being scoped.
