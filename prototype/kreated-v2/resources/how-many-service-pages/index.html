RESOURCES / HOW MANY SERVICE PAGES

How many service pagesyour website actually needs.

One page per thing you want more of, and a location page only where you can write something true about that place. Page count is not the goal and never was.

The short answer

Two rules, and they resolve most of it.

Give a service its own page when someone searches for it by name and would expect a page about it. Keep it as a section when it is a detail of something else. Most local service businesses land somewhere between four and ten service pages, not twenty-five and not one.

The test is not how many services you offer. It is how many of them a customer would type into a search box on their own, without prompting.

A page, or a section

The distinction that decides most of the list.

  • Give it a page when the intent is materially different Someone searching for water heater replacement and someone searching for drain cleaning are not the same customer with the same urgency and the same budget. Two intents, two pages.
  • Keep it a section when it is part of the same job Permit handling, cleanup, or a warranty are things a customer wants explained inside the service they are buying, not searched for separately. A page for each would be thin and would compete with the page that matters.
  • Combine when two pages would say nearly the same thing If you cannot write a genuinely different page for each, they are one page. Two thin pages perform worse than one good one, and they split whatever authority the topic earns.
  • Split later, not upfront Start with the services you actually want more of. A page can always be split when a section grows enough to deserve independence.

The city page question

The one most owners are really asking about.

The advice going around is to build a page for every town you serve. For most local service businesses that is wrong, and it is wrong in a way that can leave a site worse than before.

A location page earns its place when three things are true at once: you genuinely work there and want more of it, someone in that town is searching for the service, and you can write something about that town that is not the Raleigh page with a name swapped. Different neighborhoods, different building stock, a job you actually did there, a different travel or response reality.

If the third one is not true, the page is a copy with a place name in it. A search engine has seen that pattern many times, a customer notices it immediately, and it does not read as a business with local depth.

Two or three real location pages beat twenty templated ones, and that is not a cautious position, it is the one that produces results. It is also why Kreated will not build a city-page farm and says so on Web Design & Development.

Why the count matters for the buyer, not just search

The half of this that gets forgotten.

A service page is the thing you can send someone. When a customer asks whether you do a particular job, a link to a page about that job answers it completely. A homepage with nine services in a list does not.

It is also how a site stays understandable. Pages that each have one job, linked sensibly to each other, are easier for a visitor to navigate and easier for a search engine to interpret. Structure is not a search trick; it is the same clarity working in two directions at once.

The same is true for the systems that summarize businesses in an answer rather than a list of links. They are reading the same pages, and a business whose services are stated plainly in their own places is easier to describe correctly. What answer engine optimization is covers that.

Working out your own number

A short exercise, worth doing before anyone quotes.

  • List the jobs you actually want more of. Not everything you can do, what you want the phone to ring about.
  • Cross off anything a customer would not search for by name.
  • Cross off anything you could not write a genuinely distinct page about.
  • Add the towns where all three location tests hold. Usually that is one to three, occasionally zero.
  • What remains is your number, and it is nearly always smaller than the service list on the current site.
