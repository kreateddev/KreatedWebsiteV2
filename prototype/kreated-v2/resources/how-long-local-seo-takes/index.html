RESOURCES / HOW LONG LOCAL SEO TAKES

How long local SEO takesbefore you start seeing movement.

Some of it is visible in weeks. The part you are actually paying for usually is not, and knowing which is which is the difference between patience and wasted money.

The short answer

Before the variables.

For most local service businesses, early movement tends to appear somewhere between three and six months, with more meaningful change over six to twelve. Some things happen much faster than that, and confusing the fast things with the slow ones is why month two feels like failure.

Nobody can give you a date, and anybody who does is describing something they do not control. What can be described honestly is the order things happen in and roughly how long each part takes.

Four stages, routinely mistaken for one

They happen in order, and they are days to months apart.

  • 1. Discovery, hours to days Google learns the page exists. This is fast and it feels like progress. It is not yet worth anything.
  • 2. Indexing, days to weeks The page enters the index and becomes eligible to appear. Uneven across a site: some pages index in a day, others take weeks, and a partial index early on is normal rather than a fault.
  • 3. Ranking, weeks to months The page starts appearing for searches, at some position. Position 40 is ranking. It produces no visits, and it is still the stage where you can see whether the work is pointed at the right things.
  • 4. Traffic and calls, last Someone actually clicks, and later, someone actually calls. This is the only stage the business feels, and it arrives well after the first three.

Most of the frustration in month two is a business at stage three judging itself by stage four.

What changes the speed

Two businesses doing identical work will not move at the same rate.

  • How old the site is and what it has already earned. A domain with years of history moves faster than one registered last month. This is not something you can buy your way past.
  • The condition of the Google Business Profile. An unverified or half-filled profile is often the single largest and fastest fix available, which is why it is usually done first. See Google Business Profile.
  • How contested your market is. A pool leak detection business on a stretch of coast competes with a handful of others. A general contractor in a metro competes with hundreds. Same work, different timelines.
  • Whether the pages that need to rank exist yet. A site with one page covering nine services has nothing to rank for eight of them. Until those pages exist, nothing about them can move.
  • Whether the content is worth ranking. Thin pages written to fill a slot do not perform, and adding more of them does not help.
  • Whether the work is actually continuous. Three months of attention followed by six months of nothing does not compound. This is the most common reason a business concludes SEO does not work for them.

What can genuinely happen in the first few weeks

Real, and worth having.

A profile that was never verified gets verified. Categories and services that were wrong get corrected. Hours, service area and phone number stop disagreeing between the site and the listing. Pages that existed but could not be read properly become readable. New service pages get published and indexed.

These are real improvements and some of them can affect what a customer sees quite quickly, particularly anything on the profile. What they are not is a change in competitive position. That takes longer, because it depends on things that accumulate.

What actually takes months

The part being paid for.

Becoming the more relevant answer than the businesses currently above you is not a switch. It comes from having the right pages, having them be genuinely useful, having the business information agree everywhere it appears, and continuing to do that while competitors do not.

There is no shortcut in that list, and the businesses that win it are usually the ones that kept going for twelve months rather than the ones who spent more in the first three.

What would actually indicate something is wrong

As distinct from normal slowness.

  • Pages still not indexed after a month Slow indexing is normal. A page that Google has crawled and declined to index a month later is worth investigating.
  • No impressions at all after two to three months Not no clicks. No impressions. That means the pages are not being considered for anything, which is a different and more serious problem than ranking low.
  • Impressions only for your own business name Branded searches finding you proves very little. If nothing non-branded appears after several months, the work is not pointed at what people actually search.
  • Nobody can tell you what was done The clearest warning sign, and it has nothing to do with timelines. Work you cannot see described is work you cannot evaluate.

Before starting the clock

Two things worth settling first.

If the pages that need to rank do not exist yet, that is the first cost and the first delay. Working out how many pages you actually need covers how to decide that without building forty of them.

And if you are not sure whether the profile or the website is the weaker piece, local SEO and your Google Business Profile are not the same thing works through which to fix first.
