RESOURCES / LOCAL SEO VS GOOGLE BUSINESS PROFILE

Local SEO and your GoogleBusiness Profile are not the same thing.

One is a surface Google shows. The other is the system that decides what appears on it and everywhere else.

The short answer

In one paragraph.

Your Google Business Profile is one listing: the panel with your name, hours, services, area, photographs and reviews. Local SEO is the wider work of making a business findable in searches where location decides the result, and the profile is one part of it alongside your website and the information published about you elsewhere.

They get confused because the profile is the part an owner can see and edit. It feels like the whole thing. It is the most visible piece of something larger.

What the profile does on its own

More than owners expect, and less.

  • It answers the questions asked before anyone visits your site Are you open, where are you, what is the number, do people rate you. For a large share of local searches this is the entire interaction. The customer calls from the panel and never reaches your website.
  • It carries reviews, which do a lot of the deciding Nothing on your own site substitutes for them, because a buyer knows you wrote your site and did not write the reviews.
  • It states categories, services, hours and service area Incomplete or wrong here is a common, quiet, fixable problem.

What no profile can do is guarantee where it appears. Nobody controls that. Anyone promising a position is describing something they cannot deliver.

What the profile cannot carry

Where the website has to do the work.

A profile has a handful of short fields. It cannot explain a service in enough depth to answer a real question, and it cannot hold a page for each of the nine things you do. Someone searching for a specific service, rather than a category, is looking for a page about that service, and only a website has room for one.

It also cannot cover places properly. A profile has one address and a service area setting. If you genuinely work across several towns and want to be relevant in each, that relevance lives in pages on your own site, not in the listing.

And it cannot make you credible on its own. A profile with good reviews pointing at a website that looks abandoned is a mixed signal a careful buyer notices.

Which to fix first

By what you already have.

  • No profile, or one you have never claimed Start there, before anything else. It is the cheapest visibility available to a local business and it is often the reason a business with a decent website is still invisible. Google Business Profile covers claiming, verification and completing it properly. If you believe you have a profile and it still is not appearing, the reasons a business goes missing from Maps is the place to start.
  • A complete profile and a thin or missing website The profile is doing all the work and has hit its ceiling. What is missing is somewhere to explain the services and cover the places, which is a website problem before it is a search problem.
  • Both exist and neither produces much Check whether they agree before spending on either. Mismatched hours, a service area claimed in one place and not the other, a phone number that changed on one of them. A customer notices the inconsistency and doubts both; Google has no way to decide which is authoritative. It is the least glamorous fix available and it costs nothing but an hour of attention.
  • Both are in order and you still want more This is the point at which ongoing work is worth paying for, rather than before it. That is Local SEO: the continuing work on pages, relevance and the information published about the business. Before committing to anything monthly, how long local SEO takes sets out what to expect and when.

How they reinforce each other

Why doing one well makes the other work harder.

The profile and the site are read together. When the services listed in the profile have real pages behind them, when the area you claim matches the places your site actually covers, and when the name, address and number are the same everywhere, everything you publish points the same direction.

When they disagree, the disagreement itself is the problem. That is the most common thing worth fixing in a local presence, and it is neither a website project nor a marketing campaign. It is an afternoon of making two things match.

Finding out where you stand

And what this cannot tell you.

The Free Website Audit reads your public website and reports on local visibility among the six areas it covers: whether your market is named, whether location and service pages exist, and whether the site states the business information clearly enough to be read.

It does not inspect your Google Business Profile. It has no access to your Google account and cannot see your categories, your review profile or how your listing is performing. The result says so plainly rather than implying otherwise. For the profile itself, someone has to look at it with you.

If the wider question is why the phone is quiet, your website gets visitors and no calls covers all six areas.
