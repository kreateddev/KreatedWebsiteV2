RESOURCES / NOT SHOWING ON GOOGLE MAPS

Why your business may not beshowing on Google Maps.

Most causes are ordinary and fixable. One of them is not fixable by anyone, and it is worth knowing which is which before paying someone to solve it.

The short answer

Start here.

A business that does not appear is usually unverified, incomplete, miscategorized, misconfigured for its service area, restricted, or simply further from the searcher than the businesses being shown. The first five are fixable. The last one is not, by anyone.

Before assuming the worst, check the obvious thing: search for your business by name, from a device that is not signed into your own Google account. If it appears by name but not for the service you offer, that is a relevance problem, not a missing-listing problem, and they have completely different fixes.

The causes, most common first

Distinguishable, and each has its own fix.

  • The profile was never verified The most common reason of all, and the most easily missed. A profile can be created and sit unverified indefinitely. Until verification completes, it may not appear at all.
  • The information is incomplete No categories, no services listed, no hours, a missing or wrong phone number. An empty profile gives Google very little reason to show it for anything.
  • The primary category does not match what people search A restoration contractor categorized as a general contractor is competing in the wrong set. The primary category does a large share of the work and it is one field.
  • The address or service area is configured wrong A business that works at customer locations is set up differently from one customers visit. Getting this wrong can hide a business, or show it in the wrong places, and it is a settings problem rather than a marketing one.
  • The listing is suspended or restricted It happens, sometimes after an edit to the address, name or category. A profile that used to appear and abruptly stopped is the pattern to look for here.
  • Duplicate listings competing with each other An old profile from a previous owner, a previous address, or an auto-generated one. Two listings for one business split the signals.
  • The site and the profile disagree The services listed in one do not match the other, or the area does, or the number. When the two disagree there is no authoritative version, and a system deciding what to show has to pick one or trust neither.
  • Nothing is wrong and you are simply further away Proximity is a real factor and nobody controls it. A searcher in a town twenty minutes away is often shown businesses in that town. This is the cause most often misdiagnosed as a problem, and no amount of spending fixes it.

Why nobody can promise you a position

Including Kreated.

What appears depends on who is searching, where they are standing, what they typed, and what Google has decided about every business nearby. Two people on the same street can see different results. Anyone offering a guaranteed position is either describing something they cannot control or selling a screenshot from one search on one device.

What can be controlled is whether the profile is complete, accurate, correctly categorized and consistent with the website. That is real work with a real effect, and it is the honest version of the offer. It is Google Business Profile.

If the profile is fine and you still are not found

Then it was never the listing.

A complete profile has a ceiling. It has a handful of short fields and cannot explain a service in depth or cover several towns properly. If you are visible for your business name and for one obvious category but nothing else, what is missing is usually pages on your own site.

That is the wider work: Local SEO. The distinction between the two is worked through in where the profile ends and local SEO begins, and how long any of it takes is in how long local SEO takes.

What the audit can and cannot check

Stated up front so it is not mistaken for more.

The Free Website Audit reads your public website. On the local side it reports whether your market is named, whether service and location pages exist, and whether the site states the business clearly enough to be read and matched.

It cannot see your Google Business Profile. It has no access to your Google account, so it cannot tell you whether the profile is verified, suspended, duplicated or miscategorized. Those need someone signed in, looking at it. The audit result says which things it did not inspect rather than implying it knows them.
