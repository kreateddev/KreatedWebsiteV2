What it is

Before any of the detail.

Brand strategy is deciding what the business is, who it is for and what it says. Identity is the mark, the type, the colour and the rules that carry that decision everywhere the business appears.

For a contractor or a local business the identity is not a logo on a website. It is a truck, a yard sign, a quote document, a shirt, an invoice and a phone screen — usually all before anybody visits the site. Work that only considers the screen produces a mark that fails on the truck.

Three levels, and the honest difference

What each one is actually for.

A refresh. The existing mark cleaned up or redrawn, keeping what people already recognize. This is for a business that is already known locally under its current mark: the recognition is an asset, and starting over would spend it rather than build on it. It is not a new identity, and if the mark should not stay recognizably itself, this is the wrong rung.

A new identity. A logo system rather than a single lockup — the versions you actually need for a sign, a truck, an invoice and a screen. This is for a business changing what it is called, what it sells, or how seriously it wants to be taken. It is also the right starting point when the current mark is something made quickly years ago and tolerated since.

A full identity system. Everything in a new identity, plus the market and competitor research that stops the result being a matter of taste. This suits a business with several services, several audiences, or plans to hire people who will need to represent it.

Figures for all three are on the pricing page, with what each includes.

What this is not

Stated plainly, because it matters.

Not a rebrand campaign. Announcing a new identity — the launch, the messaging around it, the sequencing — is a separate decision and a separate piece of work.

Not a logo in isolation. A single image with no system behind it produces the situation this work exists to fix: a mark that works in one place and is quietly redrawn by whoever needs it somewhere else.

Not required before a website. Brand work can run alongside a build or entirely on its own. Where the two run together they are quoted together but priced as two things, so you can see what you are buying.

Questions

Do I need branding, or just a logo?

If you need one file for one purpose, you need a logo. If the same business has to look like itself on a website, an estimate, a van and a sign, you need the decisions around the logo too, the type, the color, and the rules for using them. A Brand Refresh from $750 is the smaller version of that.

Can I get branding and a website together?

Yes, and it is usually the cheaper order of operations. Brand + Website typically runs $3,950–$5,950+ depending on brand scope, website package and copy needs. Doing the identity first means the site is built from settled decisions rather than provisional ones.

Will you write my tagline?

Only if there is something true to say. A messaging foundation is part of Brand Identity, and that covers how the business describes itself. A slogan invented to fill space under a logo is not an asset.

We already have a logo. Is it wasted?

Usually not. A Brand Refresh exists for exactly this: cleaning up or refining what you have, settling the typography and color around it, and writing the short guide that stops it drifting again.

How is this priced, and how is it paid?

Brand Refresh from $750, Brand Identity from $1,500, Full Identity System from $2,500+, and Brand + Website typically $3,950–$5,950+. Where branding runs alongside a website project, the standard terms apply: 50% to start and 50% before launch, with larger or custom engagements using milestones defined in the proposal. Everything is scoped and priced in writing first.

Do I own the finished identity, and do I get the source files?

After final payment you own the finished work and the design and content created specifically for you. Ask, and you get the project files that go with it, the vector logo files, the working files, and the exports you need to hand to a printer or a sign maker. Two things cannot come with them, and no studio can hand them over: commercial typefaces and stock imagery are licensed to a named party, so those licences are bought in your name rather than transferred. And Kreated keeps its own reusable internal systems, tooling and components. Nothing specific to your business.

What is the difference between brand strategy, brand identity and marketing?

Brand strategy decides who the business is for, what it stands for and what position it is trying to own. Brand identity expresses those decisions visually and in words. Marketing puts the resulting offer in front of people. They work in that order. An identity built without the strategy underneath looks considered and says nothing in particular.

When should a business rebrand rather than refresh?

Refresh when the business is still the same business and the presentation has aged. Rebuild the identity when what the business does, who it serves or what it claims has genuinely changed, and the current identity now describes a company that no longer exists.
