What it is

Before any of the detail.

Local SEO is the work of making a business findable at the moment somebody nearby is looking for what it does — in the map results, in the ordinary search results, and in the answers that increasingly sit above both.

It is not one task. It is a website that explains the business clearly, a Google Business Profile that is accurate and complete, pages that exist for the services and places you actually serve, and the listings that agree with each other. Those things move together or not at all, which is why they are sold together here rather than as a menu.

Most of the decision happens before anyone calls. Somebody searches, reads three results, and picks one. Local search work is about being in that set and being the one that reads as the safest choice.

What Kreated actually handles

The parts, named.

The Google Business Profile. Categories and services refined, description, service area, hours and photos corrected, and the details that are quietly wrong put right. For most local buyers the profile is what they see before the website and often instead of it, so getting it accurate is usually the highest-return hour anyone spends on local search. It is available as a one-time correction, or as part of ongoing work.

The pages that carry a service or a place. A dedicated service page lets one service be found and compared on its own terms instead of being a line on a list. A location page does the same for a market you genuinely serve. Both are only worth building where there is enough that is specifically true to fill them — see what this is not, below.

The site itself. Structure, internal linking, the words on the page, and the technical setup that lets all of it be read correctly. A site that is confusing to a person is confusing to a search engine for most of the same reasons.

Measurement. Analytics and meaningful conversion events configured so the things that count as a lead are actually recorded as one. That turns “we get some traffic” into a number you can act on: how many people got in touch, from where, and which pages did the work.

Who it’s for

And who it is not for.

Contractors and home-service companies are the specialty — remodelers, builders, plumbing, HVAC, electrical, roofing, restoration, leak detection, pool and concrete. Businesses that already do the work well and want more of it coming in. The same work suits any local business whose customers search before they call.

Three shapes of engagement, and the honest difference between them:

Maintenance. A business already visible enough that the job is keeping it that way. The foundation stays accurate, the profile stays fed, and nothing decays quietly. This is not a programme of new pages.

Continuous work. A business visible enough to be worth defending, in a market where competitors are actively working on the same thing. Full existing-page SEO, ongoing keyword research and conversion work belong here rather than in maintenance.

Several services or several cities. Where organic visibility is a growth channel rather than a hygiene factor, and where the volume of new work each month can actually be justified by demand.

Figures for all three are published on the pricing page, along with what each one includes and what it deliberately does not.

What this is not

Stated plainly, because it matters.

Not advertising. Nothing here buys clicks. Paid search can be a sensible thing to run alongside this work, but it is a different budget with a different behaviour, and it stops the day you stop paying for it.

Not a ranking promise. No position is guaranteed, by Kreated or by anyone who tells you otherwise. What is offered is the work, done properly, and reporting honest enough that you can judge it.

Not mass-produced location pages. A page for every town within fifty miles is a well-known tactic and a bad one. A location page that earns its place needs genuine local relevance, real service relevance, unique and useful content, sensible internal linking, a considered search intent and a QA pass. Where those are not there, the page is not built.

Not a subscription you cannot leave. Ongoing work is billed monthly in advance. The continuous tier carries a three-month initial commitment and is month-to-month after that; the other two have no documented initial term.

Questions

How is this different from the SEO in a website project?

A website project launches the site search-ready: titles, headings, clean URLs, a sitemap and Search Console, plus keyword mapping and internal linking on a Growth build. That is the foundation, and it is finished when the site launches. Local SEO is the work that continues afterward, adding pages where demand appears, keeping the structure coherent as the business changes, and watching what search actually does with it.

Will you guarantee a position on the first page?

No, and be careful with anyone who does. Positions are decided by Google, they move, and no agency controls them. What can be committed to is the work: the pages, the structure, the relevance signals and the monitoring.

How many location pages will you build?

As many as there is real demand for, and no more. A Growth website project includes up to two service or location pages where the demand supports them; the ongoing program adds others as they are justified. A page for every town within an hour’s drive is a well-known way to make a site worse.

Do I need this if I already have a good website?

Not automatically. If the site is well structured and you are already found for the work you want, the honest answer is to leave it alone. It is worth doing when there are searches you should be answering and no page that answers them.

What does it cost, and is it monthly?

The foundations are built inside a website project, a Growth build at $2,950 includes the keyword research, the mapping and the technical groundwork. The continuing program is Local Growth at $900/month, on a three-month initial commitment and month-to-month after that. Every engagement is scoped and priced in writing before it starts.

What do I actually get each month?

Reporting covers the work completed, changes in search and visibility, any relevant observations about the profile and the search results, the next priorities, and anything that needs a decision from you. Where something is not measurable, it is not reported as though it were. There is no ranking promise, no lead target and no growth percentage attached to the program. What is committed to is the work and an honest account of it.

How long before anything changes?

For most local service businesses, early movement tends to appear between three and six months, with more meaningful change over six to twelve. Competitive categories take longer. Profile activity such as calls and direction requests usually moves before any position does. That is a general pattern rather than a promise about a particular business, and it assumes the foundations are in place first.

Does my website or my Google profile do more of the work?

They do different jobs. The website carries service and location relevance, the content that answers what people search for, and the structure connecting it. The profile carries the business presence in maps and local results: categories, hours, services, reviews and local prominence. Neither substitutes for the other, which is why they are priced separately .
