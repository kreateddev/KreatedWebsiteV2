What it is

And which part does which job.

Local SEO is the work of making a business more visible in searches where location decides the result, by improving the website, the business information published about it, and the local relevance of both.

Two things do the work, and they are not the same thing.

The website carries service relevance, location relevance, the content that answers what people search for, the internal structure that connects it, and information that can actually be crawled and read.

The Google Business Profile carries the business presence itself: how it appears in maps and local results, its categories, its hours and services, its reviews, and the signals of local prominence attached to it.

Neither substitutes for the other. A strong profile attached to a site that never explains what the business does is a limit. A well structured site with an incomplete profile is also a limit.

Timing. For most local service businesses, early movement tends to appear somewhere between three and six months, with more meaningful change over six to twelve. Competitive categories take longer. Profile activity usually moves before any position does, which is why it is worth watching first. None of that is a guarantee, and the numbers above describe a general pattern rather than a promise about a particular business.

What Local SEO actually means here

A specific job, not a monthly mystery.

Local search is decided by three things at once: whether a page exists that answers the search, whether the site makes it obvious you work in that place, and whether the business looks the same everywhere someone checks.

So the work is structural before it is anything else: which pages exist, what each one answers, how they link to each other, and what a search engine can tell about where you operate. It is the system around the website, which is why it is priced as a program rather than folded into a build.

The profile itself is a separate service: Google Business Profile.

Who it’s for

Local search is worth doing when proximity actually decides the sale.

  • Businesses whose customers are nearby and searching now Trades, restoration, home services: work that is searched for at the moment it is needed and compared in minutes.
  • Businesses that serve an area rather than a storefront If the van covers thirty towns, one page saying “we serve the region” is not the same as pages that answer each place people actually type.
  • Businesses with a site that was never structured for search Everything on one page, or fifteen pages competing for the same search. The fix is architectural, not cosmetic.

What this is not

Where the edges are.

  • Not a ranking guarantee. Nobody can promise a position, and nothing on this site claims one.
  • Not the Google Business Profile itself. That is its own service, and the two are priced separately.
  • Not mass-produced city pages. Location pages are added where genuine demand supports them; thin duplicates of the same page with the town swapped are the thing this work exists to avoid.
  • Not Google Ads. Paid placement is a different budget and a different service.
  • Not a one-off. The foundations are built inside a website project; the continuing work is monthly, because search results are not static.

Questions

How is this different from the SEO in a website project?

A website project launches the site search-ready: titles, headings, clean URLs, a sitemap and Search Console, plus keyword mapping and internal linking on a Growth build. That is the foundation, and it is finished when the site launches.

Local SEO is the work that continues afterward, adding pages where demand appears, keeping the structure coherent as the business changes, and watching what search actually does with it.

Will you guarantee a position on the first page?

No, and be careful with anyone who does. Positions are decided by Google, they move, and no agency controls them. What can be committed to is the work: the pages, the structure, the relevance signals and the monitoring.

How many location pages will you build?

As many as there is real demand for, and no more. A Growth website project includes up to two service or location pages where the demand supports them; the ongoing program adds others as they are justified.

A page for every town within an hour’s drive is a well-known way to make a site worse.

Do I need this if I already have a good website?

Not automatically. If the site is well structured and you are already found for the work you want, the honest answer is to leave it alone. It is worth doing when there are searches you should be answering and no page that answers them.

What does it cost, and is it monthly?

The foundations are built inside a website project, a Growth build at $2,950 includes the keyword research, the mapping and the technical groundwork. The continuing program is Local Growth at $900/month, on a three-month initial commitment and month-to-month after that.

Every engagement is scoped and priced in writing before it starts.

What do I actually get each month?

Reporting covers the work completed, changes in search and visibility, any relevant observations about the profile and the search results, the next priorities, and anything that needs a decision from you.

Where something is not measurable, it is not reported as though it were. There is no ranking promise, no lead target and no growth percentage attached to the program. What is committed to is the work and an honest account of it.

How long before anything changes?

For most local service businesses, early movement tends to appear between three and six months, with more meaningful change over six to twelve. Competitive categories take longer. Profile activity such as calls and direction requests usually moves before any position does.

That is a general pattern rather than a promise about a particular business, and it assumes the foundations are in place first.

Does my website or my Google profile do more of the work?

They do different jobs. The website carries service and location relevance, the content that answers what people search for, and the structure connecting it. The profile carries the business presence in maps and local results: categories, hours, services, reviews and local prominence.

Neither substitutes for the other, which is why they are priced separately.
