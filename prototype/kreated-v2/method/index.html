How the workactually runs.

Five steps. What happens in each, where you sign off, and what is needed from you. Because the schedule is shared whether or not anyone says so out loud.

The five steps

Each one ends with something you approve. Nothing moves forward on an assumption.

  1. Understand

    A conversation about the business before the website: what the work is, who buys it, how they find you now, and what currently goes wrong between someone finding you and someone calling.

    You receive
    A written account of what is wrong and what it is costing, in plain language rather than an audit dump.
    You approve
    Nothing yet. This is diagnosis.
    Needed from you
    An honest account of what is not working, and access to anything that already exists.

  2. Decide

    Scope becomes a written document: which pages exist, what each one is for, what is included, what is explicitly not, and what it costs. Most website projects take about 2–4 weeks from here, depending on scope, feedback, and how quickly the content and assets arrive.

    You receive
    A scope in writing: what is being built, what is not, in what order, at what price.
    You approve
    The scope and the price, in writing, before any work begins.
    Payment
    50% to start. Larger or custom projects may use milestones defined in the proposal.

  3. Build

    Structure first, then design, then the build, by the same person, so what is drawn is what ships. Responsive from the start rather than adapted afterward. Copy is worked through with you as the pages take shape rather than poured in at the end.

    You receive
    The site itself, designed and built, plus the copy work agreed in the scope.
    You approve
    The page structure, then the design, then the copy.
    Needed from you
    Photographs, service details, and timely answers. This is the step where a slow week costs a week.

  4. Verify

    Checked across real screen sizes and browsers. Measurement is wired in before launch, not after, GA4 with form submissions and phone taps recorded as conversions, along with titles, headings, clean URLs, a sitemap and Search Console.

    You receive
    A launched site with its search foundations, tracking and profile work in place, checked rather than assumed.
    You approve
    The finished site, before it goes live.
    Payment
    The remaining 50%, before launch.

  5. Improve

    A Growth project includes a review about 30 days after launch: indexing, tracking, and any early friction. Market Leader adds a second at about 60 days. Beyond that, continuing work is a separate decision rather than an automatic renewal.

    You receive
    A record of what changed and what it did, and the next thing worth doing.
    You approve
    Whether anything continues at all.
    What is committed to
    The work, and a straight account of what it did. Positions in search are Google’s to give, and nobody can promise them.

What changes by service

The five steps hold. What sits inside them does not.

  • Web Design and Website Redesign, the full five steps as written above. A redesign spends less time in Understand, because a great deal is already answerable by looking at what exists.
  • Local SEO, Decide and Build happen inside a website project; Improve becomes the ongoing program rather than a review. Local Growth is $900/mo on a three-month initial commitment, month-to-month after.
  • Google Business Profile, a one-time pass through the profile inside a Growth project. It is not monthly management, and Verify means checking the profile and the website agree.
  • Brand Strategy, Understand and Decide carry more weight, because the decisions made there are the deliverable. Build produces the system rather than a site.
