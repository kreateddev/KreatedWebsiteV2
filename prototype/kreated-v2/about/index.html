What Kreated is

A one-person practice that designs and builds websites, and the local search and brand work around them, for businesses whose online presence has fallen behind the quality of their actual work.

There are six services. Most businesses need two or three of them, and the reason they sit together is practical rather than philosophical: the same decisions keep coming up in each one, and splitting them across three vendors is usually why the last attempt did not hold together.

Who it works with

Documented from real engagements, not from a target persona exercise.

  • Local service businesses Contractors, restoration, trades: work that is searched for urgently, compared quickly, and won by whoever looks most capable.
  • Businesses with a site that has aged out Something that was fine to launch with and now argues against the quality of the work it is supposed to represent.
  • Founders and small practices Where the person you talk to is the person who does the job. That cuts both ways, and it is why this practice is built the same way.

The problem it exists for

The customers this work is for are usually better at their job than their online presence suggests. The pain is not “I need a website”. It is being judged as smaller and less capable than you are, by someone who spent forty seconds deciding.

That is a solvable problem, and it is mostly not an aesthetic one. It is structure, clarity, and being findable at the moment somebody is looking.

What to expect

  • You deal directly with the person doing the work No account manager in between, no brief passed to someone who wasn’t in the room, no explaining your business twice.
  • Scope and price in writing, before anything starts Standard website projects are 50% to start and 50% before launch. Most take about 2–4 weeks, depending on scope, feedback, and how quickly the content and assets arrive.
  • You own what was made for you After final payment, the finished website and the design and content created specifically for your project are yours.
  • Nothing gets claimed that cannot be shown There are no invented numbers anywhere on this site. Where a result exists it is labelled and dated; where it does not, the page says so. Work is where that is easiest to check.

What this is built on

A view about what a website is for.

A website is judged on more than how it looks. It has to be found, understood, trusted and acted on.

Appearance is the part that is easiest to see and easiest to sell. It is also the part that gets checked first and argued about most. But a site that looks considered and cannot be found, or reads well and never explains what the business actually does, has not done its job.

The parts that decide whether a site earns anything are mostly the ones that are not obvious from a screenshot. Whether someone can tell within seconds what the business does and whether it covers them. Whether the next step is clear enough to take on a phone. Whether the information is structured so search engines can index it and, increasingly, so the systems that summarise businesses describe it correctly rather than guessing. Whether the site says the same thing as the Google profile and everywhere else the business appears.

None of that is exotic. It is ordinary work that is frequently skipped, because it is invisible in a portfolio shot and hard to charge for as a separate line item. It is included here because leaving it out produces a site that photographs well and underperforms quietly.

The practical consequence is how the work is scoped. Design decisions are made alongside the structure, the copy and the way the site will be found, rather than after them. One person carries all of it, so those decisions do not get separated from each other.
