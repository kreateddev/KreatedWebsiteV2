# AUDIT RESEARCH — the Free Website Audit

How buyers perceive a free audit, and what would make this one work.

🚫 **No audit landing page is written or designed here.** 🚫 The audit is not to be designed or
implemented yet ([`../docs/DECISIONS.md`](../docs/DECISIONS.md) 010).

Evidence grades per [`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md).

---

## 1. Does the format work? — yes, and unusually well

**Evidence: MEDIUM-HIGH (C11).**

- Audit tools "work especially well for SaaS and consulting businesses as lead magnets"; HubSpot's
  Website Grader has been "a brand-defining magnet for 15+ years" (C11).
- Audits "convert well, often above 40% on targeted traffic, and qualify leads deeply" (C11).
- A well-targeted lead-magnet page converts at **20–50%** when the offer matches the audience (C11).
- **Specificity increases conversion** — *"Get My Free 30-Minute Audit" outperforms "Book a Call"* (C11).
- **Audit-sourced leads convert faster** than benchmark-report leads (C11).
- The exact use case is named: "a web development agency offering a free 30-minute review of a
  visitor's current site attracts exactly the people who are in the market for what they sell" (C11).

⚠️ Grade C — vendor-published benchmarks with a commercial interest in high conversion figures.
Treat 40%+ as directional, not as a forecast.

🔴 **The strongest argument for the audit is not its conversion rate.** It is that the primary
competitor is **doing nothing** ([`COMPETITIVE_ALTERNATIVES.md`](COMPETITIVE_ALTERNATIVES.md) §1),
and the only thing that beats inertia is making an invisible loss visible. **The audit is the only
mechanism in Kreated's offer that does diagnosis before commitment.**

---

## 2. 🔴 The problem: everyone already offers one

**Evidence: HIGH (E1, E2, E4).**

Three of four Raleigh competitors lead with a free audit or its equivalent:

| Competitor | Offer |
|---|---|
| Perry SEO (E1) | "Get My Free SEO Audit" — plus *"No obligation, no pressure — just actionable insights"* |
| Tyndall (E2) | "Get My Free Ranking Plan" |
| NC Digital (E4) | "Request a Free Consultation" |

**The free audit is a category convention, not a differentiator.** A buyer who has seen three of
these reads the fourth as the same thing.

⚠️ **Direct consequence for [`../docs/DECISIONS.md`](../docs/DECISIONS.md) 010:** retaining the audit
is correct, but **the offer itself carries no competitive advantage.** Any advantage has to come from
what the audit actually *is* and what it *delivers* — not from offering one.

## 3. What makes "free audit" sound spammy

**Evidence: MEDIUM (C10, D2, E1–E4) plus reasoning from the trust deficit.**

No study of "free audit" perception specifically was found. Assembled from the adjacent trust
literature and from what the market has trained buyers to expect:

| Spam signal | Why |
|---|---|
| **It is obviously a sales call in disguise** | "Free consultation" (E4) is understood as a pitch. The buyer's cost is not the audit — it is the call they now have to escape |
| **Automated output with no human judgment** | Generic scanner reports are a known genre and read as worthless. ⚠️ Sharp risk given the internal AI association ruled out of public naming (DECISION 010) |
| **Manufactured urgency or scores** | "Your site scores 34/100!" is a pressure device. **>60% of agency clients end up unhappy** (C10) — this market recognises the technique |
| **Asking for too much before giving anything** | Phone number, budget, company size before any value |
| **Findings that are obviously templated** | If it could describe any site, it describes nothing |
| **No stated limit** | The genre never says what it will not tell you. ⚠️ Nobody in the sample states limits — the one habit Kreated already has |
| **The unsolicited-outreach association** | *"I get five of those calls a week"* — inferred VOC. "Free audit" is the standard opener of exactly the cold callers this buyer already deflects |

🔴 **The last one is the real risk.** "Free website audit" is the most-used opening line of the
cold-outreach industry. **The phrase itself is contaminated** — which is a live consideration given
that the name is now fixed.

## 4. What would make it genuinely appealing

**Evidence: MEDIUM. Derived, not directly measured.**

| Property | Basis |
|---|---|
| **It tells them something they did not know about their own business** | The buyer has the symptom without the cause — 73% not confident their marketing works (B8); deals lost in unseen private research (C1) |
| **It is verifiable without trusting Kreated** | GBP has an owner-facing dashboard. **"Here is your own number, go look"** is unforgeable. **59 actions/month is the benchmark** to compare against (B2) |
| **It is specific to them, visibly** | Specificity is the measured conversion driver (C11) |
| **It costs almost no time** | **56% of SMBs have ≤1hr/day for marketing** (B8). ⚠️ This is the binding constraint |
| **It states what it will not tell them** | Nobody else does ([`COMPETITOR_MESSAGING.md`](COMPETITOR_MESSAGING.md) "What nobody says" #2) |
| **It has a defined end** | "30-minute" outperforms open-ended (C11) — a bounded thing cannot become a sales sequence |
| **It is useful even if they never hire Kreated** | The single strongest anti-spam signal available, and the only one competitors cannot copy without changing their business |

## 5. What they expect to receive

**Evidence: LOW-MEDIUM. Largely inferred — flagged accordingly.**

Ranked by likely value to the buyer:

1. **"Here is what a customer sees before they call you"** — the private-research view they never get
   (C1). Highest value, nobody provides it.
2. **Their own Google Business Profile numbers, in context** — verifiable, benchmarkable (B2).
3. **What is losing them the comparison, named specifically** — reviews under threshold (**68% now
   require 4+ stars**, A1), slow mobile load (**53% leave after 3s**, B4), no recent reviews (**74%
   want reviews from the last 3 months**, A1).
4. **What to fix first, and what not to bother with** — ⚠️ the "what not to bother with" half is the
   credibility-maker; it proves the audit is not a sales instrument.
5. **Roughly what fixing it involves** — partially defusing the cost freeze (C8) without a quote.

⚠️ **All five are inferences from behavioural evidence. None was tested with buyers.**

---

## 6. What commitment anxiety it removes — and does not

**Removes:** the price freeze (nothing to buy yet), the time cost (if genuinely short), the fear of
being sold five services (nothing is scoped yet), and the risk of being wrong about their own
problem.

**Does not remove:** the fear of a sales call, the fear of being added to a list, or scepticism that
anything free is worth having.

🔴 **The audit removes the *purchase* decision but replaces it with a *contact* decision.** For a
buyer whose scarcest resource is attention, that is not automatically a smaller ask. **A free audit
requiring a scheduled call is not low-friction — it is a differently-shaped commitment.**

---

## 7. What must NOT be promised

Hard limits from [`../docs/PROOF.md`](../docs/PROOF.md). Non-negotiable.

- 🚫 **No ranking promise, guarantee or projection.** Kreated cannot bypass, guarantee or control
  Google's decisions.
- 🚫 **No invented metrics, scores, percentages or traffic figures** — including any auto-generated
  "score" not backed by real measurement.
- 🚫 **No implied team** ("our analysts will review…").
- 🚫 **No results-you-can-expect framing.** LearnSmart's outcome is client-reported and bounded.
- 🚫 **No naming the LLEC result surface** — it is undocumented.
- ⚠️ **No AI-capability implication.** DECISION 010 removed "AI Audit" from public positioning; the
  audit must not imply an automated engine that does not exist.
- ⚠️ V1's locked labels — `Nothing is scanned right now.` · `Nothing has been scanned yet.` ·
  `Early access is opening soon.` — were written for an unbuilt engine and are **not automatically
  correct** for a manually-delivered audit ([`../docs/SERVICES.md`](../docs/SERVICES.md) §4).

## 8. 🔴 The unresolved question — is the audit the primary conversion?

[`../docs/CONVERSION.md`](../docs/CONVERSION.md) §1 inherits V1's ordering: `Start Your Project`
primary, audit secondary.

**The research points the other way**, for four reasons:

1. The primary competitor is **doing nothing**, and only diagnosis beats inertia (§1).
2. The buyer **cannot self-diagnose** (B8, C1), so "Start Your Project" asks for a decision they are
   not equipped to make.
3. **Time is the binding constraint** (B8) — the smaller first step wins.
4. `Start Your Project` is **not distinctive**; NC Digital uses "Start Your Website Project" (E4).

⚠️ **Counter-evidence, stated fairly:** the audit is a category convention (§2), the phrase is
contaminated by cold outreach (§3), a "free audit" leading to a call may not be low-friction (§6),
and — decisively — **whether the audit can be delivered at all today is unknown**
([`../docs/BUSINESS.md`](../docs/BUSINESS.md) §9). An audit promoted to primary conversion **must be
deliverable at volume, by one person, every time.**

🔴 **This is a Skyler decision, not a research conclusion.** Recorded in
[`STEP_2_SYNTHESIS.md`](STEP_2_SYNTHESIS.md) §16. 🚫 Step 2 does not change conversion priority.

## 8a. 🔴 REVISION 2026-08-19 — what F1 changes about the audit

First-party founder recall (**F1**, [`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md)) both strengthens and complicates the
audit's role.

### Strengthened — "Send me some information" is a request for exactly this
**Evidence: 🟢 F1 item 5.**

One of the most common responses Kreated hears is not an objection at all. *"Send me some
information"* is a **request for a low-commitment artefact** — precisely what the audit is.

This gives the audit a second job Step 2 had not identified: it is not only an inbound conversion
device, it is **the answer to the most common cold-call outcome.**

### 🔴 Complicated — the direction of travel is wrong

*"Send me some information"* means **you send, I receive.**
The audit as conceived asks the prospect to **submit their URL first, then receive.**

Those are opposite. A prospect who asked to be *sent* something and is instead handed a form to fill
in has been given homework, not information — and **time is a Stage 1 objection**
([`OBJECTIONS.md`](OBJECTIONS.md) S1.2).

⚠️ **This suggests two distinct artefacts may be needed** — one **sendable** without prospect action,
one **requestable** on the site. They may share content; they are not the same object. 🚫 Not resolved
here. Skyler decision, recorded in [`STEP_2_SYNTHESIS.md`](STEP_2_SYNTHESIS.md) §16.

### 🔴 Complicated — "audit" is threatening to a contented buyer

**Evidence: 🟢 F1 item 4, F1 items 7–8.**

The word *audit* presupposes something might be wrong. Delivered to a prospect who has just said
*"we're consistent / content with where we're at"* or *"we already have a website"*, it proposes to
find fault with a position they have stated and a decision they have already made
([`OBJECTIONS.md`](OBJECTIONS.md) S1.1, S1.4).

⚠️ **The name is fixed** as `Free Website Audit` ([`../docs/DECISIONS.md`](../docs/DECISIONS.md) 010)
and this is **not** a request to change it. The finding is about **what the audit must contain**:

- **Checkable facts, not verdicts.** Their own GBP numbers against the **59 actions/month** benchmark
  (B2); their own review recency against the **74% who want reviews under three months old** (A1);
  what a customer actually sees. All verifiable by the buyer without trusting Kreated.
- ⚠️ **Confirming that something is fine must be a possible outcome.** An audit that can only find
  problems is a sales instrument, and this buyer recognises the genre. An audit that can return "your
  profile is healthy, your reviews are current, here is the one thing slipping" is credible precisely
  because it could have said nothing was wrong.

### Unchanged
- Offering an audit is **not** a differentiator — 3 of 4 competitors already do (§2).
- The phrase remains contaminated by cold outreach (§3) — ⚠️ **and F1 sharpens this**, since Kreated's
  own cold calls are part of what contaminated it.
- Nothing in §7 (what must never be promised) changes.
