## 0. The segmentation logic

Skyler's brief: *"Especially businesses where one additional customer/job can have meaningful
value. Do not assume every local business is equally attractive."*

That is the correct axis, and the research supports building the whole segmentation on it. Two
numbers set the boundary:

- **66.3% of small businesses spend under $1,000/year on marketing** (C7). A business in that
  majority cannot buy a website at any agency price band.
- **The SBA benchmark is 7–8% of gross revenue to marketing**; SMB actuals run 5–10% (C7).

Those combine into a hard filter. At 7% of revenue, a **$500k** business has ~$35k/yr of total
marketing capacity; a **$150k** business has ~$10k — and a website consumes most of a year's budget.
Agency websites run **$2,000–$8,000 typical, $15,000+ for advanced** (C9); Raleigh-local competitors
publish **$1,500–$5,000+** (E2) and **$500–$7,000+** (E3).

**Conclusion (high confidence): the qualifying variable is not industry. It is (job value × close
rate) against a revenue base large enough to fund a four-figure project without pain.**

Trade economics make this concrete (C3, C4):

| Trade | Job / customer economics |
|---|---|
| HVAC | Service-only customer **$1,200–3,500** CLV; new install **$8,000–15,000**; maintenance-plan customer **$47,200**. Average CLV **$15,340**. CAC **$296–350** |
| Roofing | Margins 34–42%; repeat rate only **5–15% over 10 years** — every job must be won cold |
| Restoration | ⚠️ No CLV data found. Reasoned from job type: urgent, insurance-adjacent, high ticket |

A roofer or restoration contractor who wins **one** additional job may cover an entire website. An
HVAC company that converts one service customer into a maintenance-plan customer gains ~$45k of
lifetime value. **That is the arithmetic that makes a website purchase rational, and it is the
arithmetic Kreated's site has to let the buyer do in their own head.**

## 1. PROFILE A — The Established Trade Operator *(primary)*

**Confidence: HIGH.** Best-evidenced segment; matches two of Kreated's three real clients.

| Attribute | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Business type | Restoration, roofing, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, pool/leak services, remodeling, specialty trades | C2, C3, C4 · matches Rare Raleigh + LLEC |
| Size / maturity | Established, past survival stage. Owner-operated with a small crew. ⚠️ Exact employee count not evidenced; **82.3% of US small businesses are nonemployer firms** (C7), so "small crew" is already the upper half of the market | C7 |
| Decision-maker | **The owner, personally.** Not a marketing manager — one does not exist. **47% of owners handle all their own social media**; **56% have an hour or less per day for marketing** | C7, B8 |
| Marketing sophistication | Low to moderate. **73% of SMB owners are not confident their marketing is working** — the defining psychological fact about this buyer | B8 |
| Website situation | Has one. It is old, slow or built on a DIY builder. **Average mobile page loads in 8.6 seconds** against Google's 3-second threshold; **53% of mobile visitors leave after 3 seconds** | B4 |
| Lead generation | Referral-dependent and proud of it. Referrals are **~20% of a balanced lead mix but close at 40–70% at $0–52/lead** — genuinely their best channel, which is exactly why they under-invest elsewhere | C5 |
| Budget psychology | **The freeze.** Owners "know they need something better than a Facebook page but also know that an agency quoting $50,000 is solving a different problem than the one they have" — so they do nothing | C8 |
| Urgency triggers | See [`PAINS_AND_TRIGGERS.md`](PAINS_AND_TRIGGERS.md) | — |
| Why they start looking | Something external forced the comparison — a lost job, a competitor, a referral that didn't convert | C1, C8 |

### What makes this segment commercially attractive
1. **One job pays for the project.** The ROI conversation is short.
2. **Their customer's behaviour is now brutally search-mediated** — **98% of consumers search online
   to find local businesses**, up from 90% in 2019 (C7). The owner can feel this happening.
3. **Their competitors are visibly winning on exactly the axis Kreated sells.**
4. **They already have proof of demand** (referrals). Kreated is adding a channel, not creating one
   — a far easier sale than "make my business work."

### What makes this segment hard
- Time-poor and hard to reach.
- **Deeply sceptical.** **>60% of brands are unhappy with their agency within the first year**
  (A6-adjacent, C10); **38% switch SEO providers annually**, citing "lack of transparency" and
  "inability to see what we're paying for" (C10).
- Prefers phone over form.
- 🔴 **Often the bottleneck is not marketing.** **95% of 466 home-services companies did not respond
  to a lead within five minutes; 40% never responded at all** (C6). Sending more leads into an
  unanswered phone produces no result and an unhappy client. **This is a qualification question, not
  a copy problem.**

## 2. PROFILE B — The Credibility-Constrained Professional Practice *(secondary)*

**Confidence: MEDIUM.** Supported by Kreated's LearnSmart engagement and by credibility research;
less supported by segment-level survey data.

| Attribute | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Business type | Solo or small professional practice — coaching, tutoring, therapy, consulting, specialty health, boutique legal/financial | LearnSmart (project fact) |
| Size / maturity | Small, often one practitioner. Revenue capacity is the constraint, not demand |  |
| Decision-maker | The practitioner. Also the person doing the work, the invoicing and the marketing |  |
| Marketing sophistication | Low, but **design-literate** — they can see that their presence undersells them |  |
| Website situation | Frequently **none at all**, or a single social profile. **~27% of US small businesses still have no website**; of those, **34% say it is "not relevant to my industry"**, 26% cite cost, 21% use social instead | B6, B7 |
| Lead generation | Referral and reputation. Growth is capped by the practitioner's own network |  |
| Budget psychology | Cautious but not paralysed. Buys once, properly, then leaves it alone |  |
| Why they start looking | A credibility event — raising rates, a new location, a partnership, or being asked "do you have a website?" once too often |  |

### Why this profile matters more than its size suggests
- **It is where Kreated's only testimonial and only complete case study come from.** Missy Boyd's
  review names *branding and website together* and praises collaboration and a **two-week**
  turnaround ([`../docs/PROOF.md`](../docs/PROOF.md) §5).
- Web Design (no existing site) and Brand Strategy attach naturally here; both are in Kreated's five.
- The sale is emotionally easier: the practitioner already believes they are better than they look.

### Why it is secondary, not primary
- Lower ticket, lower repeat, weaker one-job-pays-for-it arithmetic.
- ⚠️ **Direct tension with [`../docs/DECISIONS.md`](../docs/DECISIONS.md) 007:** LearnSmart — the
  proof for this segment — is deliberately not a homepage Work default. Documented in
  [`STEP_2_SYNTHESIS.md`](STEP_2_SYNTHESIS.md) §15 for Skyler.

## 3. PROFILE C — The Newly Serious Operator *(tertiary / opportunistic)*

**Confidence: LOW–MEDIUM.** Real, but under-evidenced as a distinct segment.

A business that recently crossed a threshold — new ownership, a rebrand, a second location, a first
employee, or a jump in revenue — and whose brand and site no longer match what it has become.

| Attribute | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Structural change in the business, not dissatisfaction with the website | ⚠️ inferred |
| Decision-maker | Owner, often newly so |  |
| Sophistication | Variable, but **motivated** — a rebrand has an internal deadline |  |
| Budget psychology | **Best of the three.** Money is already allocated to "the change" |  |
| Risk | Timeline pressure; may want everything at once |  |

Attractive because the buyer arrives pre-sold on *needing* something and is choosing *who*, not
*whether*. ⚠️ Kreated has no documented engagement of this type; the profile is reasoned, not
observed.

## 4. Anti-profile — who is NOT the buyer

Documented because attracting these costs real time. Detail in
[`BEST_FIT_CLIENT.md`](BEST_FIT_CLIENT.md).

| Not the buyer | Why |
|---|---|
| The sub-$1,000/yr marketing majority | **66.3% of small businesses** (C7). No agency at any price serves them. AI builders now do — and **93–95% of AI-builder users are first-time website owners with no prior paid web presence** (B1) |
| Pure price-shoppers | Will find a $500 Google Site locally (E3) |
| Businesses wanting guaranteed rankings | Kreated cannot and must not promise this ([`../docs/PROOF.md`](../docs/PROOF.md) §6) |
| Ecommerce / product businesses | Different problem, different stack, no evidence Kreated serves it |
| Businesses whose customer is worth under ~$100 | The arithmetic never closes |
| Businesses needing a team, 24/7 support or account management | Founder-led solo cannot honestly serve this |
| Businesses that will not answer the phone | **95% fail the 5-minute response test** (C6). Kreated's work cannot survive this |

## 5. What is still unknown about the buyer

- ❓ Actual revenue band of Kreated's real buyers — never recorded.
- ❓ Whether the buyer is genuinely Raleigh-local or geography-agnostic. LLEC is Wilmington;
  LearnSmart is Raleigh/Durham/Chapel Hill. **The evidence so far is regional, not city-local.**
- ❓ Where Kreated's existing clients actually came from — referral, search, outreach? No analytics
  ever existed (limitation 3 in [`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md)).
- ❓ Whether owners in this market buy after one visit or several.
- ❓ Whether spouses/partners are co-deciders — plausible in owner-operated trades, **unevidenced**.
