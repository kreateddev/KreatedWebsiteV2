# PERSUASION SEQUENCE

**What must be believed before the next thing can be believed.**

🚫 This is not a page design, not a section list, not a layout, and not a wireframe. It is a belief
order. How many sections it becomes, what they contain and how they look is Step 3+ work.

Evidence grades per [`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md).

---

## Why the example order in the brief was not used

Skyler's brief offered `relevance → problem recognition → value → credibility → services → proof →
risk reduction → conversion`, with the instruction not to use it unless the research supports it.

**It does not survive contact with three findings:**

1. **Credibility is not stage 4. It is stage 0** — decided in ~50ms, before any content is read
   (A3, A4, A5). Placing it fourth assumes the buyer withholds credibility judgment until given
   evidence. They do not; they have already made it.
2. **"Value" cannot be argued at stage 3.** The buyer arrives with hard-won scepticism (C10) and
   discounts value claims from everyone. Value must be *earned* by demonstrated diagnosis, not
   asserted early.
3. **"Proof" is not a single stage.** The research shows two different proofs doing two different
   jobs at two different moments: *diagnostic* proof (you understand my problem) early, and
   *capability* proof (you have built real things) later. Collapsing them wastes the first.

## 🔴 REVISION 2026-08-19 — entry contexts and the express path

First-party founder recall (**F1**, [`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md)) does **not** change the order below.
It adds two requirements the original sequence did not account for.

### Requirement A — the sequence has four entry points, not one

The original sequence assumed one visitor arriving cold at stage 1. F1 item 5 (*"Send me some
information"*) and item 9 (immediately eager) reveal at least four:

| Visitor | Arrives at | Needs | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Referred** | ~Stage 4 | Not to be disqualified. Confirmation, not persuasion | A6, C5 |
| **Search** | Stage 1 | The full sequence | C7, E1–E4 |
| **Post-cold-call** — *"send me some information"* | Stage 1, mid-conversation | Proof the call was worth taking; the promised information, actually delivered | 🟢 **F1 item 5** |
| **Eager** — decided already | **Stage 7** | A fast path to contact, immediately | 🟢 **F1 item 9** |

🔴 **The post-cold-call visitor is a new audience Step 2 had not modelled.** They have already spoken
to Skyler, are mildly interested, and are checking whether they were sold to. **The website is
literally stage two of a sales conversation that has already begun** — a role no other research in
Step 2 anticipated.

⚠️ **A directional asymmetry worth flagging:** *"send me some information"* means **you send, I
receive.** The Free Website Audit asks the prospect to **submit** something first
([`AUDIT_RESEARCH.md`](AUDIT_RESEARCH.md)). Those are opposite directions of travel. A **sendable**
artefact and a **requestable** one may not be the same object. 🚫 Not resolved here — flagged for
Skyler in [`STEP_2_SYNTHESIS.md`](STEP_2_SYNTHESIS.md) §16.

### Requirement B — an express path must exist at every stage

**Evidence: 🟢 F1 item 9, corroborated hard by C6 and C1.**

Some prospects are immediately eager. And a rejection-derived list **systematically under-records
them** — eager prospects end calls quickly and pleasantly ([`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md) F1) — so their
true share is likely higher than the list suggests.

Their own industry sets their expectations: **78% of customers buy from the first business to
respond**; **85% of homeowners choose the first contractor who responds** (C6, C1). **This buyer's
market punishes friction ruthlessly, and they apply the same standard to Kreated.**

🔴 **A sequence optimised for the sceptic taxes the eager buyer — who is the most valuable visitor on
the site.** The fix is **not** a shorter sequence, which would fail the sceptic. It is a **bypass
available at every stage**: the ready buyer must never have to complete education to reach contact.

🚫 **This is a structural requirement, not a design instruction.** It does not specify a sticky
header, a floating button, a repeated CTA or any other mechanism. Step 3+ owns how.

### What did NOT change

- **The order.** Nothing in F1 contradicts it.
- **Stage 2 remains the hinge.** F1 strengthens it — see the amendment in Stage 2 below.
- **It remains a belief order, not a scroll order.**

## The sequence

### STAGE 0 — "This was made by someone who knows what they are doing"
**Pre-verbal. ~50 milliseconds. Not a section — a property of the whole artefact.**

**Evidence: HIGH (A3, A4, A5).** Credibility judged in 50ms, correlating strongly with 500ms
judgments; 94% of first-impression feedback concerns design; identical content is judged more
credible with better presentation.

**What must be believed:** *this is competently made.*
**How it is proved:** by the site existing and being excellent. Nothing else.

🔴 **The gate for everything below.** If Stage 0 fails, the honest bounded evidence in Stage 4 reads
as excuses rather than rigour. **A web design agency's site is the only argument that gets evaluated
before it is read.**

---

### STAGE 1 — "This is for a business like mine"
**Evidence: HIGH (A6, E1–E4).**

**What must be believed:** *these people work with businesses like mine, near me.*

Every competitor clears this in their H1 (E1–E4). It earns nothing and its absence is fatal.

⚠️ Complication: Kreated's evidence is **regional, not city-local** — Wilmington and
Raleigh/Durham/Chapel Hill. Honest relevance here is slightly weaker than a competitor's
unqualified "Raleigh."

---

### STAGE 2 — "They can see something about my situation that I can't"
**Evidence: HIGH (C1, B8, C10, and the absence in E1–E4).**

**What must be believed:** *they understand my problem better than I can state it.*

🔴 **The hinge of the entire sequence, and the largest opening found in Step 2.**

Three facts converge:
- **73% of SMB owners are not confident their marketing is working** (B8) — symptom without cause.
- **Deals are lost during the customer's private research**, which the owner never observes (C1).
- **Nobody in the competitor sample does this** ([`COMPETITOR_MESSAGING.md`](COMPETITOR_MESSAGING.md)
  "What nobody says" #1, #3). All four offer audits; none demonstrates diagnosis on the page.

Why it must precede everything commercial: a sceptical buyer discounts every claim about outcomes,
but **cannot discount an accurate description of their own situation.** Being correctly diagnosed is
**self-verifying** — checked instantly against their own experience, at zero risk, requiring no
trust in Kreated at all.

🔴 **This is also the only stage that beats the primary competitor.** The primary competitor is
**doing nothing** ([`COMPETITIVE_ALTERNATIVES.md`](COMPETITIVE_ALTERNATIVES.md) §1), and inertia is
broken by making an invisible loss visible — not by an offer.

### ⚠️ AMENDED 2026-08-19 — how diagnosis must arrive

F1 confirms this stage's importance and **raises its difficulty.** The obstacle is not only *"they
cannot see it"* — it is also *"they do not want to look."* Kreated's real prospects state a position:
*"We're consistent / content with where we're at."*

🔴 **An unrequested diagnosis delivered to someone who has declared themselves content is received as
an accusation** ([`OBJECTIONS.md`](OBJECTIONS.md) S1.1). The same is true of the binary-possession
belief — *"we already have a website"* — where telling them their site is bad attacks a decision they
already made (S1.4).

**The distinction that resolves both:**

| ❌ Reads as accusation | ✅ Reads as information |
|---|---|
| Your website is failing you | Here is what your Google Business Profile did last month, against the average of **59 actions** (B2) |
| You are losing customers | **74% of people now want reviews from the last three months** (A1) — here is when yours were written |
| You need to fix this | Here is what a customer sees before they call you |

**Checkable facts, not judgements.** The buyer verifies each against their own dashboard or their own
eyes, at zero risk, without having to trust Kreated or concede that they were wrong.

⚠️ **This is a strategy note about the nature of the evidence, not about wording, tone or layout.**
🚫 The right-hand column is illustration of the *mechanism* — it is not copy and is not approved.

### STAGE 3 — "That problem is costing me something I care about"
**Evidence: MEDIUM-HIGH (C3, C4, C5, C8) + 🟢 F1 item 4.**

**What must be believed:** *the arithmetic justifies acting.*

### ⚠️ AMENDED 2026-08-19 — the payoff is not always growth

F1 item 4 (*"We're consistent / content with where we're at"*) shows that **a meaningful share of
good-fit prospects do not want more volume.** Their own economics support them: an HVAC
maintenance-plan customer is worth **$47,200** against **$1,200–3,500** for service-only (C3) —
**customer quality outperforms customer quantity by more than 10×.**

🔴 **All four sampled competitors sell growth** — *"grow revenue"*, *"dominate"*, *"attract more
customers"* (E1); *"More Calls from Google Maps"* (E2); *"Technology That Helps Small Businesses
Grow"* (E4). **Every one is pitched at a goal a real segment of good-fit buyers does not hold.**

Other legitimate payoffs this buyer may want instead: **credibility** · **better-quality
opportunities** · **professionalism** · **protecting existing referral flow** · **fewer bad-fit
enquiries to sift**.

⚠️ **Consequence for this stage:** it cannot assume the unit of value is *more jobs*. For the
contented operator the arithmetic that matters is **job quality, not job count** — and possibly
**time saved**, given that time is a Stage 1 objection ([`OBJECTIONS.md`](OBJECTIONS.md) S1.2).

🔴 **This is the largest unclaimed position found anywhere in Step 2.** Recorded as a finding.
🚫 Not a concept, not a positioning decision, not copy — Step 3 owns that.

Must come **after** Stage 2 — a cost cannot be sized before the problem is recognised.

The buyer's own economics do this work, not Kreated's claims: HVAC service-only CLV **$1,200–3,500**
vs maintenance-plan **$47,200**; average CLV **$15,340** against CAC **$296–350** (C3). Roofing
margins **34–42%** with only **5–15% repeat over ten years** (C4) — every job won cold.

⚠️ **This is where the cost freeze is defused, and it is defused by *their* numbers, not by price
disclosure.** The freeze (C8) is caused by not knowing what world they are in. A buyer who has just
worked out that one job covers the project has resized the decision themselves.

🚫 **Stage 3 must not become a results claim.** Kreated has no metrics
([`../docs/PROOF.md`](../docs/PROOF.md)). The arithmetic belongs to the buyer's business.

### STAGE 4 — "They have actually done this, for real businesses"
**Evidence: MEDIUM-HIGH (A6).**

**What must be believed:** *this is not theory. Real things exist.*

"Case studies, client tenure, and consistent reporting do more to build confidence than flashy
pitches" (A6) — an engagement *explained*, not a screenshot grid.

Deliberately placed **fourth, not second.** Proof offered before the problem is recognised answers a
question the buyer has not yet asked. Proof offered after diagnosis confirms a belief already
forming.

🔴 **Kreated's weakest stage, structurally.** Of the two approved homepage Work candidates
([`../docs/DECISIONS.md`](../docs/DECISIONS.md) 007, 008): **LLEC** is real and live but has no
case-study narrative by design; **Rare Raleigh** has the narrative but no completed implementation
and may never imply one. The project that would serve this stage best — LearnSmart — is off the
homepage by decision.

⚠️ **Possible inversion worth Skyler's attention:** if Stage 2 is done well, Rare Raleigh becomes
*stronger* here than it looks, because a documented diagnosis-before-build engagement is literally
proof of the Stage 2 capability. **Rare Raleigh proves diagnosis. LLEC proves delivery.** Together
they cover the sequence — which is a better fit for DECISION 007 than it first appears.

---

### STAGE 5 — "A specific person is responsible, and will finish"
**Evidence: HIGH as a criterion (A6); MEDIUM for the completion fear (C8, C12); 🔴 NOT differentiating
(E2, E3).**

**What must be believed:** *someone accountable will do this and it will end.*

Two beliefs, one stage:
- **Accountability** — necessary, and already claimed by competitors in plainer words: *"You won't
  be passed to account managers"* (E2), *"You get my cell phone number"* (E3). **Clear the bar; do
  not build on it.**
- **Completion** — Kreated's structural weak spot ([`OBJECTIONS.md`](OBJECTIONS.md) #7, #9): solo
  operation carries the freelancer's bus-factor fear at agency-adjacent prices.

⚠️ Two under-used counter-assets already in Kreated's materials: LearnSmart finished **start to
finish in two weeks** (client's own unprompted words), and Rare Raleigh's incomplete state is
**openly labelled** rather than hidden.

---

### STAGE 6 — "There is a defined way this happens, and I won't be trapped"
**Evidence: MEDIUM (A6, C10, E1, E3).**

**What must be believed:** *there is a method, and an exit.*

Process appears in agency-selection criteria (A6) but always behind people, transparency and
evidence. No-lock-in language is universal (E1, E3) — its absence is noticed, its presence is not
credited.

⚠️ **This is where the five-service connection argument belongs — and nowhere earlier.** *These are
one decision* is genuinely differentiating after diagnosis and indistinguishable from an upsell
before it ([`OBJECTIONS.md`](OBJECTIONS.md) #10).

⚠️ **Demotion flagged:** V1 led with the Kreated Method. The research places method at stage 6 —
supporting, not headline. Recorded in [`STEP_2_SYNTHESIS.md`](STEP_2_SYNTHESIS.md) §15.

---

### STAGE 7 — "The next step is small enough that I'll take it now"
**Evidence: HIGH (B8, C11, C6).**

**What must be believed:** *this costs me almost nothing to start.*

**56% of SMBs have ≤1hr/day for marketing** (B8); **75% find agency selection time-consuming** (A6).
Specificity converts — *"Get My Free 30-Minute Audit" outperforms "Book a Call"* (C11).

⚠️ **The bounded first step is the mechanism, and the audit is the obvious candidate** — but its
scope, deliverability and priority are unresolved ([`AUDIT_RESEARCH.md`](AUDIT_RESEARCH.md) §8).

⚠️ Mirror-image finding: this buyer's own customers punish slow response — **78% buy from the first
responder** (C6). Whatever the step is, **the reply must be fast**, and Kreated's one testimonial
already names responsiveness as a reason to buy.

## Three properties of this order

1. **It is not reorderable.** Each stage supplies the precondition for the next. Stage 3 cannot
   precede Stage 2; Stage 6 cannot precede Stage 2 without reading as an upsell.
2. **It front-loads what Kreated is strongest at** (0 and 2 — execution and diagnosis) and
   **back-loads what it is weakest at** (4 and 5 — portfolio depth and continuity). That is not
   avoidance; it is the order the evidence supports, and the alignment is fortunate.
3. **It is a belief order, not a scroll order.** Several stages may resolve simultaneously, in one
   element, or non-linearly. 🚫 **Nothing here mandates eight sections stacked in eight screens.**
4. ⚠️ **It is not a gate.** *(Added 2026-08-19.)* The order describes what a *persuadable* visitor
   must come to believe. It must never become a corridor the ready buyer is forced to walk. Property
   3 and the express path together mean the sequence can be satisfied without ever being *traversed*.

## What this sequence does NOT determine

🚫 Section count · layout · hierarchy · what the hero is or whether one exists · typography ·
colour · imagery · motion · copy · the creative concept.

**All of that is Step 3 and later, and remains entirely open.**
