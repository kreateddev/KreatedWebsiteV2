# PAINS AND TRIGGERS

What causes this buyer to finally act. **Ranked by evidence strength, not by how compelling the
story is.** Evidence grades per [`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md).

---

## Ranking method

Each trigger is scored on two independent axes:

- **Evidence** — how well the underlying phenomenon is documented
- **Trigger force** — how directly it converts into "I need to call someone", which is a separate
  question and mostly *not* evidenced by the same sources

🚫 A well-evidenced phenomenon is not automatically a strong trigger. Slow page load is extremely
well documented and almost never the thing that makes an owner pick up the phone.

---

## TIER 1 — Strong evidence, strong trigger force

### 1. Losing the comparison during the customer's private research
**Evidence: HIGH (A1, C1). Trigger force: HIGH.**

The buyer's customer now runs a multi-source check before making contact. **97% read reviews; 41%
always do** (A1). Homeowners increasingly start with an AI tool, validate names through reviews,
check the website, then contact a shortlist (C1). **98% of consumers search online to find local
businesses**, up from 90% in 2019 (C7).

The consequence is the important part: **most deals are lost during private research, not in the
sales conversation** (C1). The owner never sees the loss. They experience it as "the phone is
quieter" with no cause attached.

🔴 **This is the most commercially important finding in Step 2.** The buyer's pain is *invisible to
them*. They cannot diagnose it. Someone has to show it to them — which is precisely what a
diagnostic audit does. See [`AUDIT_RESEARCH.md`](AUDIT_RESEARCH.md).

### 2. A referral that didn't convert
**Evidence: MEDIUM-HIGH (A1, C5, C1). Trigger force: HIGH.**

Referrals are this buyer's best channel — **~20% of a balanced lead mix, closing at 40–70% at
$0–52/lead** (C5). **92% of consumers trust recommendations from friends and family more than any
advertising** (C5).

But the referred prospect still checks. When a warm referral cools after looking someone up, the
owner feels it as a personal, specific loss — the one form of this pain that is **visible,
attributable and infuriating**.

⚠️ The chain "referral → checks site → doesn't call" is **inferred**, not directly measured. The
component facts are each well-evidenced; the causal sequence is reasoned. Grade the claim
accordingly and never state it as measured.

### 3. Review-standard inflation
**Evidence: HIGH (A1). Trigger force: MEDIUM-HIGH.**

Consumer thresholds moved sharply in a single year (A1):

| Threshold | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Require minimum 4 stars | 55% | **68%** |
| Require 4.5+ stars | 17% | **31%** |
| Star rating factors into decision | — | **92%** |
| Want reviews from the last 3 months | — | **74%** |
| Want reviews from the last 2 weeks | — | **32%** |

**47% will not consider a business with fewer than 20 reviews.** Recency now outranks volume.

A business that was acceptable last year can fail this year's filter **without changing anything**.
That is a genuine, dated, external shift — and it is squarely Google Business Profile territory,
one of Kreated's five services.

## TIER 2 — Strong evidence, moderate or indirect trigger force

### 4. The AI-search rupture
**Evidence: HIGH (A1, A2). Trigger force: MEDIUM — and rising.**

Consumers using AI for local business recommendations went from **6% to 45% in one year**. **42% now
trust AI recommendations as much as written reviews**; more trust AI for local recommendations (40%)
than distrust it (32%). **Google's share of local discovery fell from 83% to 71%** (A1, A2).

This is the single largest measured behavioural shift in the dataset.

⚠️ **Trigger force is rated only MEDIUM deliberately.** Owners do not yet know this is happening.
It is a powerful *diagnostic* finding and a weak *inbound* trigger — nobody is searching "why am I
invisible in ChatGPT."

🔴 **Offer gap flagged for Skyler:** two of the four Raleigh competitors already sell against this —
Perry SEO leads with "dominate Google & AI search" (E1); Peak City asks "show up when locals search
Google or AI?" (E3). **None of Kreated's five services names it.** Not something Step 2 may fix —
services cannot be renamed or added ([`../docs/SERVICES.md`](../docs/SERVICES.md) §7). Recorded in
[`OFFER_FIT.md`](OFFER_FIT.md) and [`STEP_2_SYNTHESIS.md`](STEP_2_SYNTHESIS.md) §16.

### 5. Mobile failure
**Evidence: HIGH (B4, B5, C7). Trigger force: LOW-MEDIUM.**

**88% of "near me" searches happen on mobile** (C7). **Average mobile page load is 8.6 seconds**
against Google's 3-second threshold; **53% of mobile visitors abandon after 3 seconds** (B4). Mobile
bounce is **51.8% vs 39.7% desktop — a 12.1-point gap that has not closed in three years** (B5).

Very well evidenced. Weak trigger — the owner looks at their site on their own phone, on wifi, with
it cached, and it seems fine. **Only becomes a trigger when someone shows them.**

### 6. Google Business Profile underperformance
**Evidence: HIGH (B2, B3). Trigger force: MEDIUM.**

The average profile generates **59 actions/month — 20 website clicks, 16 direction requests, 10
calls** (B2). **56% of profile actions are website visits**, 24% calls, 20% directions. **Map-pack
businesses get 126% more traffic and 93% more calls, clicks and direction requests than positions
4–10** (C7). Profiles with photos get **42% more direction requests and 35% more website clicks**
(B2). GBP actions grew **41% year over year** (B3).

Strong trigger when the owner can see their own numbers — GBP has a built-in dashboard, so **this is
the one pain the buyer can verify without trusting anyone.** That makes it unusually valuable as an
opening move.

## TIER 3 — Real, but weakly evidenced as *triggers*

### 7. Embarrassment / the credibility gap
**Evidence: MEDIUM for the mechanism, LOW as a trigger. Trigger force: HIGH when present.**

Credibility judgment happens in **~50 milliseconds** and correlates strongly with judgments at 500ms
(A3). **94% of first-impression feedback concerns design**; identical content is judged more credible
when presented with higher aesthetic quality (A4, A5).

The mechanism is peer-reviewed and solid. What is **not** evidenced is how often embarrassment
*initiates* a purchase rather than accompanying it.

⚠️ **This is the closest thing in the research to V1's "close the gap" positioning** — and it is
supported as a *mechanism*, not as a *trigger*. See [`STEP_2_SYNTHESIS.md`](STEP_2_SYNTHESIS.md) §15.

### 8. Previous agency disappointment
**Evidence: MEDIUM-HIGH as a phenomenon (C10, D2). Trigger force: NEGATIVE.**

**>60% of brands are unhappy with their agency within the first year** (C10). **38% switch SEO
providers annually**, citing lack of transparency and inability to see what they are paying for
(C10). A "cynical segment... have tried SEO and found it wanting" and start from hard-won
scepticism (C10).

🔴 **This is a trigger to *switch*, not a trigger to *buy*, and it makes the next purchase harder.**
It belongs in [`OBJECTIONS.md`](OBJECTIONS.md) as much as here.

### 9. Growth beyond DIY branding
**Evidence: LOW. Trigger force: MEDIUM (assumed).**

**64% of US small businesses default to website builders over custom development** (B1) — so the
population of DIY-outgrowers is large. Whether outgrowing DIY *initiates* an agency search is
**not evidenced**. Reasoned, not observed. Maps to Profile C.

### 10. New location / new service launch
**Evidence: LOW. Trigger force: MEDIUM (assumed).**

Plausible and commonly claimed; no supporting research found. Recorded so it can be tested with
Skyler's own client history rather than assumed.

## 🔴 REVISION 2026-08-19 — the trigger model was half right

First-party founder recall (**F1**, [`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md)) corrects the psychology underlying
this file without changing the trigger ranking.

**What was assumed:** the buyer is *unaware* — they have the symptom without the cause.

**What F1 shows:** at first contact the buyer reports **no symptom at all.** *"We're consistent /
content with where we're at."* *"Most of our business is word of mouth."* *"We already have a
website."* Nothing in Kreated's real conversations expresses the phone being quiet, frustration with
a website, or embarrassment.

### The corrected model — untriggered vs. triggered

| | **Untriggered** (most of the market) | **Triggered** (the buyer who arrives) |
|---|---|---|
| Their position | *Consistent. Content. Word of mouth. Already have a site* | Something specific has gone wrong |
| Evidence | 🟢 F1 first-party | Secondary research; Kreated's real engagements |
| Where Kreated meets them | Cold outreach | Referral, search, or "send me some information" |
| Responds to | Information they can check | Diagnosis and capability |

🔴 **Both models are correct — about different populations.** Cold outreach reaches the untriggered
majority. The website is reached mostly by the triggered minority, plus the post-cold-call visitor
sitting between the two.

**Three consequences:**

1. **The trigger ranking below is unchanged.** It describes what *causes* the transition from
   untriggered to triggered, and F1 offers no evidence against any of it.
2. **The audience is split, and the website serves mostly the triggered.** ⚠️ Step 2 previously wrote
   as if one buyer existed in one state.
3. 🔴 **A trigger is not something Kreated can manufacture.** Nothing on a website makes a contented
   operator lose a job. What a website *can* do is be findable and convincing **at the moment the
   trigger fires** — which is why the triggers ranked below matter for timing and language, not for
   persuasion.

⚠️ **The sharpest addition from F1:** contentment is an *actively held position*, not an absence of
information. Telling a contented operator they have a problem is received as an accusation
([`OBJECTIONS.md`](OBJECTIONS.md) S1.1). **The pains below are real; asserting them at someone who has
not yet felt them is counter-productive.**

## The pattern underneath all of it

Every Tier 1 trigger shares one structure:

> **The buyer is losing business in a way they cannot see, cannot measure, and cannot diagnose
> themselves.**

They do not experience "my website is outdated." They experience *the phone being quieter than it
should be, with no explanation.*

⚠️ **Qualified by F1:** and until that happens, they experience nothing at all, and will say so.

This has one direct implication for Step 3, stated as a research finding and **not** as a creative
direction:

**Whoever names the invisible loss first, credibly and specifically, owns the conversation.** That
is a diagnosis capability, and it is the one thing Kreated has already done for a real client —
Rare Raleigh Restoration was an **audit-and-plan engagement before any build**
([`../docs/PROJECTS.md`](../docs/PROJECTS.md) §3).
