# COMPETITIVE ALTERNATIVES

The buyer is not only choosing between agencies. Each alternative below is assessed on the question
Skyler set: **why would the buyer choose it instead of Kreated?**

Evidence grades per [`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md).

---

## 1. DO NOTHING — the primary competitor
**Threat level: 🔴 HIGHEST. Evidence: HIGH (C7, C8, B8) — now upgraded by first-party F1.**

### 🔴 REVISION 2026-08-19 — "do nothing" is not passive

First-party founder recall (**F1**, [`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md)) confirms this as the primary
competitor and **changes its character.**

Step 2 originally modelled "do nothing" as **inertia** — a failure to act caused by budget, time and
invisibility. F1 shows it is often an **actively held position**, stated as a reason:

- *"We're consistent / content with where we're at."*
- *"Most of our business is word of mouth."*
- *"We already have a website."*
- *"We don't need a website."*

**These are not descriptions of paralysis. They are arguments.** The prospect has a working model of
their business, and Kreated is outside it.

⚠️ **And the argument is frequently sound.** Referrals cost **$0–52/lead and close at 40–70%** (C5);
an HVAC maintenance-plan customer is worth **$47,200** vs **$1,200–3,500** service-only (C3), so a
contractor at capacity wanting *better* jobs rather than *more* is reasoning correctly.

🔴 **Two consequences.**

1. **"Do nothing" splits into two competitors, not one:**

   | | **Passive inertia** | **Active sufficiency** |
   |---|---|---|
   | Cause | No budget, no time, cannot see the loss | A working model that excludes Kreated |
   | Evidence | C7, C8, B8 | 🟢 **F1 first-party** |
   | Beaten by | Making the invisible loss visible | ⚠️ **Not by diagnosis alone** |

2. **Diagnosis beats the first and can inflame the second.** Someone who has just said they are content
   experiences an unrequested diagnosis as an accusation ([`OBJECTIONS.md`](OBJECTIONS.md) S1.1).
   Against active sufficiency, the only non-hostile move found in the research is **information the
   buyer can verify themselves** — their own Google Business Profile numbers against the **59
   actions/month** benchmark (B2), their own review recency against the **74% who want reviews under
   three months old** (A1). **Checkable facts, not judgements.**

⚠️ Note this does not weaken the original conclusion — diagnosis is still the only thing that beats
doing nothing. It sharpens *how* it must arrive.

### Why they choose it
- **66.3% of small businesses spend under $1,000 per year on marketing** (C7). For most of the
  market, doing nothing is not a choice — it is the budget.
- **56% have an hour or less per day for marketing** (B8). Doing nothing costs zero hours.
- **73% are not confident their marketing is working** (B8). If nothing works, nothing is a rational
  allocation.
- The freeze: owners "know they need something better than a Facebook page but also know that an
  agency quoting $50,000 is solving a different problem... causing most owners to freeze" (C8).
- **The pain is invisible.** Deals lost during the customer's private research are never observed
  (C1). There is no arriving invoice for the jobs you did not hear about.

### What beats it
Only one thing in the research: **making the invisible loss visible and specific.** Not persuasion —
**diagnosis**. This is the strongest single argument for the Free Website Audit as the primary entry
point ([`AUDIT_RESEARCH.md`](AUDIT_RESEARCH.md)).

⚠️ **Every other alternative in this document is a smaller threat than this one.** A homepage
optimised to win against other agencies while losing to inertia is optimised for the wrong fight.

## 2. DIY WEBSITE BUILDERS — Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy
**Threat level: 🟠 HIGH but partly mis-framed. Evidence: HIGH (B1, B7).**

### Why they choose it
- **64% of US small businesses default to builders over custom development** (B1).
- Cost is near-zero against any agency price.
- **21% of businesses without a website say they use social media instead** (B7) — an even cheaper
  variant.
- No dependency on another person.

### Why the threat is smaller than the numbers suggest
🔴 **The most useful finding in this file:** **93–95% of AI-builder users are first-time website
builders with no prior paid web presence** (B1). Builders are overwhelmingly capturing people who
had **nothing** — not people leaving agencies.

**Builders are not primarily taking Kreated's buyers. They are creating them.** The DIY site is
stage one; dissatisfaction with it is the trigger that produces Profile A. A builder site that
disappoints is a **lead-generation event for Kreated**, delayed by two to five years.

### What beats it
Nothing Kreated says. The builder beats itself, on schedule, when the owner's business outgrows what
it can produce. **Arguing against builders is arguing with the buyer's past self and wastes the
homepage.**

## 3. AI WEBSITE BUILDERS — the newest and least understood
**Threat level: 🟠 HIGH and rising. Evidence: MEDIUM-HIGH (B1, C14, C15).**

### Why they choose it
- **58% of small businesses now use generative AI, up from 23% in 2023** (B1). SMB marketing AI
  adoption reported at **87% as of April 2026** (C7).
- Fastest adoption is among SMBs, "where cost sensitivity and speed-to-market are critical" (B1).
- Output quality is now far above the 2015 template era. The "it looks obviously cheap" counter-
  argument is expiring.

### The counter-argument that is actually emerging
**Visual sameness.** AI-generated sites show "a predominance of beige and cream backgrounds with
rusty orange accents", and their ubiquity has "turned them into instant design clichés" (C14).

🔴 **This is a genuine, dated, external market shift that favours a distinctive designed site** —
and it arrives exactly as Kreated is choosing a creative concept.

⚠️ **Recorded as a market finding, not a creative direction.** It says *sameness is becoming
visible and cheap-looking*. It does **not** say what Kreated should look like. Step 3 owns that.

### What beats it
Being visibly not-generated. Which is an execution problem, not a messaging problem.

## 4. FREELANCERS
**Threat level: 🟠 HIGH — the closest substitute. Evidence: MEDIUM-HIGH (C12).**

### Why they choose it
- Materially cheaper; "lower hourly rates and minimal overhead" (C12).
- "A good freelancer can feel less like a vendor and more like a hands-on partner, which is valuable
  when you want fast decisions and practical advice instead of long meetings" (C12).
- Direct access to the person doing the work.

🔴 **Read that quote again.** It is a near-exact description of what Kreated sells. **From the
buyer's seat, Kreated may be indistinguishable from a good freelancer** — same solo structure, same
direct access, same fast decisions — **at an agency-adjacent price.**

### Where freelancers lose
Buyer-side fears (C12): no backup if the freelancer is unavailable; "no way to supervise them and
track their progress"; may lack breadth for e-commerce, CRM or multilingual work.

⚠️ **Kreated carries most of those same fears** ([`OBJECTIONS.md`](OBJECTIONS.md) #7, #9). The
difference Kreated can honestly claim is **documented method and documented evidence standards** —
things a freelancer typically has none of, and which Kreated demonstrably does.

### What beats it
Visible process and visible standards. Not "we're an agency" — Kreated is one person, and claiming
otherwise breaks [`../docs/PROOF.md`](../docs/PROOF.md) §6.

## 5. THE LOCAL TRADITIONAL AGENCY
**Threat level: 🟡 MEDIUM. Evidence: MEDIUM-HIGH (E1–E4, A6).**

The four Raleigh competitors sampled (E1–E4) are the direct set. Full analysis in
[`COMPETITOR_MESSAGING.md`](COMPETITOR_MESSAGING.md).

### Why they choose it
- **Tenure and volume** — "15+ Years Experience", "700+ Websites Built" (E3); "helped over 150
  clients" (E2); "87%+ Retention Rate", "$10M+ Revenue Generated" (E1).
- **Published pricing** removes the freeze (E2, E3).
- **Specialization** — "Raleigh's premier SEO agency for home-service businesses" (E1).
- **Review volume** they can point at; Kreated has one review (A1's under-20 threshold applies).
- Bundled adjacent services Kreated does not sell — Google Ads, LSAs, call tracking (E1).

### Where they lose
- **>60% of brands are unhappy with their agency within the first year** (C10) — including these.
- **38% switch SEO providers annually** (C10).
- Everyone sounds the same; sameness is itself a vulnerability.

### What beats them
Not out-claiming them. **Every claim they make, Kreated can only make smaller.** The opening is
that their claims are pre-discounted by a burned market, while a verifiable specific is not.

## 6. SEO-ONLY SHOPS
**Threat level: 🟡 MEDIUM. Evidence: MEDIUM (C10).**

### Why they choose it
- Cheaper, monthly, feels lower-commitment than a rebuild.
- Directly addresses the felt symptom ("I'm not showing up").
- **Peak City sells GBP optimization at $300** (E3) — an order of magnitude below a website project
  and a very easy yes.

### Where they lose
The whole trust deficit lands here hardest — "vague retainers, hidden fees, rigid contracts, and
'black box' reports" (C10). This category burned the market.

⚠️ **Two of Kreated's five services (Local SEO, GBP Optimization) live in this category.** They
inherit its scepticism. See [`OFFER_FIT.md`](OFFER_FIT.md).

## 7. AN EMPLOYEE, FAMILY MEMBER, OR THE OWNER THEMSELVES
**Threat level: 🟡 MEDIUM. Evidence: MEDIUM (C7, B8).**

**47% of owners handle all their own social media** (C7); **82.3% of US small businesses are
nonemployer firms** (C7), so "the marketing person" is usually the owner, a spouse or a relative.

### Why they choose it
Costs nothing visible. Feels controllable. Nobody to distrust.

### Where it loses
It never finishes. The owner has **≤1hr/day** (B8) and a business to run. The half-done site is the
single most common precondition for a Kreated engagement — Peak City's positioning explicitly
addresses it: *"for businesses with... one left half-finished"* language appears on Kreated's own V1
services hub too.

## 8. REFERRAL-ONLY MARKETING — "I don't need any of this"
**Threat level: 🔴 UPGRADED to #2 (was #2 in ranking, now first-party confirmed). Evidence: HIGH
(C5, A1, C1) + 🟢 F1 first-party.**

🔴 **Confirmed 2026-08-19.** *"Most of our business is word of mouth"* is one of only four beliefs
Kreated's prospects volunteer unprompted (F1 item 6). This is **the most-stated competitive
alternative in Kreated's real market**, not an inferred one.

### Why they choose it
Because **it works, and the economics are genuinely superior**: referrals cost **$0–52 per lead and
close at 40–70%** (C5); **92% trust friend and family recommendations above all advertising** (C5).
This is not a delusion — it is the buyer's best-performing channel.

### Where it loses
- It cannot scale predictably.
- 🔴 **Referrals are checked.** The referred prospect still reads reviews (97%, A1) and still visits
  the site (C1). **The website is not competing with referrals — it is the failure point in the
  referral process.**

⚠️ That reframing is the single most useful sentence in this file for answering the "I already get
referrals" objection. Its causal chain is **inferred**, and must be stated as reasoning, never as a
measured fact.

## Threat ranking

| Rank | Alternative | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Do nothing** | Budget, time and invisibility all point here. Beaten only by diagnosis |
| 2 | **Referral-only** | Rationally defensible; requires the strongest counter-argument |
| 3 | **Freelancers** | Closest substitute; hardest to differentiate from honestly |
| 4 | **AI builders** | Rising fast; countered by execution, not argument |
| 5 | **Local agencies** | Direct, but pre-discounted by market scepticism |
| 6 | **DIY builders** | Mostly a *feeder* into Kreated's market, not a competitor for it |
| 7 | **SEO-only shops** | Cheap entry point; drags two Kreated services into a distrusted category |
| 8 | **Internal/family** | Real, but self-defeating |

🔴 **Ranks 1 and 2 — do nothing and referral-only — are not agencies.** The homepage's hardest work
is against a buyer who is not shopping, not against buyers comparing agencies.

⚠️ **Confirmed and sharpened by F1 (2026-08-19).** Both are now first-party evidenced, and both turn
out to be **positions the prospect argues for**, not states they have drifted into. The distinction
matters: a drifting buyer can be woken; an arguing buyer must be answered on their own terms, with
facts they can check rather than claims they must accept.
