# BUYING CRITERIA

What this buyer uses to judge an agency. Evidence grades per [`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md).

🚫 No criterion was assumed dominant in advance. Several that agencies believe matter most turn out
to be weakly evidenced.

---

## Tier 1 — Best evidenced

### 1. Referral and reputation — how they find you at all
**Evidence: HIGH (A6, C5).**

**Word-of-mouth and referrals remain the most common way clients discover new agencies** (A6). In
the buyer's own world the same rule holds: **92% of consumers trust recommendations from friends and
family more than any form of advertising** (C5).

🔴 **Implication that reframes the entire website brief:** a large share of qualified visitors will
arrive **already referred**. For those visitors the homepage's job is **not to persuade — it is to
avoid disqualifying**. The site is a confirmation surface before it is an acquisition surface.

This is the strongest argument in the research against a homepage that leads with a hard pitch.

### 2. The agency's own website — as evidence, not decoration
**Evidence: HIGH (A3, A4, A5).**

Credibility is judged in **~50 milliseconds**, and that judgment correlates strongly with judgments
made at 500ms (A3). **94% of first-impression feedback concerns design** (A4). Identical content is
judged more credible when presented with higher aesthetic quality — the "amelioration effect" (A5).

For a web design agency this is not vanity. **The site is the work sample.** A buyer who cannot
evaluate code, schema or search strategy *can* evaluate whether the thing in front of them feels
competently made — and will, within a twentieth of a second, before reading a word.

⚠️ **This is the one criterion where Kreated can win outright and cheaply.** It requires no clients,
no metrics, no tenure, no reviews. It requires the site to be genuinely well made.

⚠️ It also means **every proof mechanism ranked below this one is applied *after* the buyer has
already formed a credibility impression.** Ordering consequence in
[`PERSUASION_SEQUENCE.md`](PERSUASION_SEQUENCE.md).

### 3. Transparency — of price, method and reporting
**Evidence: HIGH (A6, C10).**

"Transparency is one of the most important criteria when selecting a marketing agency, requiring
clear visibility into data, team structure, and performance metrics" (A6). Businesses evaluate on
"proven results with real numbers, clear specialization..., transparent pricing tied to outcomes,
honest communication..., strategic process..., and fair contracts" (A6).

The negative case is stronger still: **38% switch providers annually** over "lack of transparency"
and "inability to see what we're paying for" (C10).

⚠️ **Note the asymmetry.** Transparency is listed as a *criterion* but functions mostly as a
*disqualifier* — its absence loses deals more reliably than its presence wins them.

### 4. The people, not the pitch
**Evidence: HIGH (A6).**

"Honesty, professionalism, and reliability from the people a business will actually work with
continue to outweigh flash or even raw experience" (A6).

🔴 **But this is table stakes in Raleigh, not a differentiator.** Competitors already claim it, some
more concretely than Kreated ever has:

- Tyndall: *"You won't be passed to account managers — you'll work with me on strategy and results."* (E2)
- Peak City: *"You get my cell phone number."* (E3)

⚠️ **"Founder-led" is the single most-contested claim in this market.** Kreated's differentiation
cannot rest on it. Detail in [`COMPETITOR_MESSAGING.md`](COMPETITOR_MESSAGING.md).

### 5. Responsiveness — as a proxy for everything
**Evidence: HIGH (C6, C1).**

**78% of customers buy from the first business to respond**; in a study of **466 home-services
companies, 95% did not respond within five minutes and 40% never responded at all** (C6). **85% of
homeowners choose the first contractor who responds** (C1). Optimal response windows are reported
compressing from 5 minutes toward 90 seconds (C6).

That data describes the buyer's *own* customers — but the buyer applies the same standard when
choosing a vendor, and their expectations are set by their own industry norms.

⚠️ Kreated's one testimonial independently corroborates this as a purchase driver:
*"never letting more than a couple of hours pass without a response, any day of the week"*
([`../docs/PROOF.md`](../docs/PROOF.md) §5). **The only first-party evidence Kreated has names
responsiveness as a reason to buy.** That is a notable convergence.

## Tier 2 — Moderately evidenced

### 6. Real work, actually live
**Evidence: MEDIUM-HIGH (A6).**

"Case studies, client tenure, and consistent reporting on performance do more to build confidence
than flashy pitches" (A6).

⚠️ Note what this says: **case studies**, not screenshots. A grid of images is weaker than one
engagement explained. Kreated has exactly one full case study — LearnSmart — which
[`../docs/DECISIONS.md`](../docs/DECISIONS.md) 007 keeps off the homepage.

### 7. Specialization
**Evidence: MEDIUM (A6).**

"Clear specialization in services you actually need" appears in the criteria set (A6).

Locally, specialization is already being claimed and narrowed: Perry SEO positions as *"Raleigh's
premier SEO agency for home-service businesses"* (E1). ⚠️ Kreated's five-service breadth reads as
generalist against that — a real positioning tension, and **not one Step 2 may resolve**, since
services cannot be renamed or dropped ([`../docs/SERVICES.md`](../docs/SERVICES.md) §7).

### 8. Reviews and ratings
**Evidence: HIGH for the buyer's customers (A1); MEDIUM by extension to agency selection.**

**68% require 4+ stars; 31% require 4.5+; 92% factor star rating; 47% will not consider a business
with under 20 reviews; 74% want reviews from the last three months** (A1).

🔴 **The mirror problem, stated plainly.** These thresholds are the buyer's own habits. Applied to
Kreated: **one review** ([`../docs/PROOF.md`](../docs/PROOF.md) §5) fails the under-20 filter that
**47%** apply, and the review is older than the 3-month window **74%** prefer.

⚠️ **This bears directly on [`../docs/DECISIONS.md`](../docs/DECISIONS.md) 017.** Star glyphs and
"Rated 5 Stars" are permitted subject to evidence and design approval. The research says showing
stars invites the very count comparison Kreated loses — **a 5-star display with one review can read
worse than no display at all.** Recorded as a finding for Skyler, not as a design decision.

### 9. Local knowledge
**Evidence: MEDIUM (E1–E4).**

Every competitor sampled foregrounds Raleigh; one claims *"100% Local & Independent"* (E3). Heavily
*used*, which suggests it is believed to work — but no buyer-side research was found showing local
knowledge is a decision criterion rather than a relevance filter.

⚠️ Kreated's evidence is **regional, not city-local**: LLEC is Wilmington, LearnSmart is
Raleigh/Durham/Chapel Hill. V1's positioning line already hedges this — *"Based in Raleigh. Built
for businesses anywhere."*

## Tier 3 — Weakly evidenced or contradicted

| Criterion | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Proof / measurable results** | Universally *claimed* — "$10M+ Revenue Generated", "372% increase in leads" (E1) — but **>60% of brands are unhappy within a year anyway** (C10). Results claims are made by everyone, including the 60% who disappoint. 🔴 **Their persuasive value is heavily discounted, which is the best news in this document for Kreated** |
| **Clear process** | Listed in criteria sets (A6) but always alongside stronger factors. ⚠️ Note: process is what Kreated leads with (the Kreated Method). Evidence supports it as *supporting* material, not as a headline |
| **Guarantees** | 🚫 **No evidence buyers require guarantees.** Some evidence that over-promising is precisely what burned them (C10). Kreated cannot offer ranking guarantees regardless |
| **Ongoing support** | Ambiguous — desired as a service, feared as a retainer trap ([`OBJECTIONS.md`](OBJECTIONS.md) #8) |
| **Speed of delivery** | Weakly evidenced as a *criterion*, strongly evidenced as *satisfaction*: the one testimonial names two weeks unprompted. ⚠️ Possible under-used asset |
| **Pricing transparency** | Genuine split. Named in criteria (A6); half the local sample publishes prices (E2, E3). No evidence on whether publishing wins or loses more deals |

## What the evidence actually says, in order

1. **Referral gets them there.** The site's first job is not losing them.
2. **The site itself is judged in 50ms** — before any claim is read.
3. **Transparency and honest people** decide who survives the shortlist.
4. **Responsiveness** closes.
5. **Results claims are discounted**, because everyone makes them and 60% of them disappoint.

🔴 **The strategic reading:** the criteria Kreated is weakest on — tenure, client count, review
volume, measurable results — are the criteria the research shows are **most discounted by
sceptical buyers**. The criteria Kreated can win on — execution quality of its own site,
transparency, responsiveness, the honest person behind it — are the ones with the **strongest**
evidence.

That is a favourable alignment, and it holds **only if the site is genuinely, visibly better made
than every competitor in the sample.** Nothing else in this document compensates if it is not.
