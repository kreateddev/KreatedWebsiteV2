## 0. F1 — recalled sales-conversation language, analysed

🚫 **Not verbatim. Not counted. Not to be quoted on the website.**

⚠️ **These are cold-call responses**, which are a different species from homepage objections — see
[`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md) F1, "The interpretation hazard". Their social function is often to end an
interruption, not to state a belief.

### The nine, sorted by how much belief they actually carry

| # | Recalled response | Information value | What it actually is |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *"I'm not interested."* | 🔴 **Near zero** | Ritual close. Says nothing about the business |
| 2 | *"I don't have time."* | 🟡 **Medium** | Both a brush-off **and** a true constraint |
| 3 | *"We already have someone doing that."* | 🟡 **Medium** | Incumbency — real, or a convenient fiction |
| 4 | *"We're consistent / content with where we're at."* | 🟢 **HIGH** | A volunteered **position**, not a deflection |
| 5 | *"Send me some information."* | 🟢 **HIGH** | Not an objection at all — a **stage transfer** |
| 6 | *"Most of our business is word of mouth."* | 🟢 **HIGH** | A volunteered **model of how their business works** |
| 7 | *"We already have a website."* | 🟢 **HIGH** | A volunteered **belief about the category** |
| 8 | *"We don't need a website."* | 🟢 **HIGH** | A stronger form of 7 |
| 9 | **Immediately eager to start** | 🟢 **HIGH — and under-recorded** | A whole buyer type |

🔴 **The most important observation about this list: cost is absent.** Not once. No "how much", no
"too expensive", no "can't afford it". Every secondary source in this research names price as the
dominant objection — the **60× local price spread** and the resulting freeze
([`OBJECTIONS.md`](OBJECTIONS.md) #1). It does not appear in Kreated's actual first contacts at all.

**The most economical explanation: the conversation dies before price is reachable.** Price is a
**stage-2 objection**, and Kreated's cold outreach rarely reaches stage 2. That single inference
restructures the objection hierarchy — see [`OBJECTIONS.md`](OBJECTIONS.md) §0.

### The seven underlying beliefs — assessed

#### B1. "What I have works well enough"
**Verdict: 🟢 CONFIRMED. The dominant belief in the list.**

Carried by items 4, 6, 7 and 8 — four of the nine. Corroborated independently: **34% of businesses
without a website say it is "not relevant to my industry"** (B7), and **73% of SMB owners are not
confident their marketing is working** (B8) yet do not act.

🔴 **The subtle and consequential part.** Step 2 previously modelled this buyer as **unaware** — they
have the symptom without the cause ([`PAINS_AND_TRIGGERS.md`](PAINS_AND_TRIGGERS.md) §1). *"We're
consistent / content with where we're at"* is not ignorance. It is a **stated preference for
stability**, and it may be:

- **genuinely true** — the business is at capacity and growth is not wanted (see B6);
- **defensive** — they suspect otherwise and do not want the file opened;
- **ritual** — a polite way to end a call.

All three are indistinguishable on a cold call, and **all three respond badly to being told they have
a problem.**

⚠️ **This modifies the diagnosis strategy, not the diagnosis conclusion.** Diagnosis remains the
opening ([`TRUST_REQUIREMENTS.md`](TRUST_REQUIREMENTS.md) §2), but the obstacle is no longer *"they
cannot see it"* alone — it is *"they do not want to look."* An unrequested diagnosis delivered to
someone who has just said they are content is an accusation.

#### B2. A website as binary possession
**Verdict: 🟢 CONFIRMED, and it is the single largest category-level misunderstanding.**

Items 7 and 8 are only coherent if *website* is a **thing you have or lack**, not a thing with a
quality that varies. *"We already have a website"* is a complete answer only under that model — the
same way *"I already have a truck"* answers a truck salesman.

Strongly corroborated: **64% of US small businesses default to builders over custom development**
(B1), **83% now have a website** (B6), and **93–95% of AI-builder users are first-time website
owners** (B1). **The market has moved from "do you have one" to "is yours losing"** — and this buyer's
mental model has not.

🔴 **Direct consequence for Kreated's offer.** Website Redesign — the best-fitting service for the
primary buyer ([`OFFER_FIT.md`](OFFER_FIT.md) §6) — **is not a category this buyer knows they are in.**
They do not think of themselves as someone who might need a redesign; they think of themselves as
someone who already solved this.

⚠️ Note what this does *not* mean: it is **not** an argument for telling them their site is bad.
Item 4 says they will experience that as an attack. The gap is between *possession* and *performance*,
and the only non-hostile way across it is evidence about their own results rather than judgement about
their taste.

#### B3. Word of mouth makes online marketing feel optional
**Verdict: 🟢 CONFIRMED as the buyer's most-volunteered reason.**

Item 6 is a **model**, not an excuse — and it is **substantially correct**. Referrals cost **$0–52 per
lead and close at 40–70%** (C5); **92% of consumers trust friends and family above all advertising**
(C5). This buyer has correctly identified their best channel.

⚠️ Step 2 already held the counter-argument ([`COMPETITIVE_ALTERNATIVES.md`](COMPETITIVE_ALTERNATIVES.md)
§8): **referred prospects still check.** **97% read reviews** (A1); homeowners validate names through
reviews and the website before contacting (C1).

🔴 **What F1 changes is the ranking, not the argument.** Previously referral-sufficiency ranked **third**
among objections. It is one of only four beliefs Kreated's prospects volunteer unprompted. **It is the
primary objection at first contact.**

⚠️ **And the counter-argument's causal chain remains inferred, not measured** — the weakest link under
the heaviest load in this research. See [`STEP_2_SYNTHESIS.md`](STEP_2_SYNTHESIS.md) §16.

#### B4. Time / management burden may rival price
**Verdict: 🟢 CONFIRMED — and F1 suggests it may exceed price.**

Item 2 appears in Kreated's real conversations; **price does not appear at all**. Corroborated:
**56% of SMBs have ≤1hr/day for marketing** (B8); **75% say agency selection is time-consuming and 77%
say it is far from simple** (A6).

🔴 **The under-examined half is the *ongoing* burden, not the purchase.** This buyer is not only
asking "what will this cost me in money" — they are asking "what will this cost me in **attention**,
forever." A website implies decisions, content, reviews, updates and someone to chase. ⚠️ Note
Kreated's one positive testimonial is built on **collaboration** — *"asked my input at every turn"* —
which is exactly the burden this buyer is refusing. **Kreated's best working method is this buyer's
stated objection.** That tension is real and unresolved.

#### B5. "Send me information" makes the website stage two of the sale
**Verdict: 🟢 CONFIRMED. Structurally the most consequential item in F1.**

Item 5 is not a rejection — it is a **handoff**. The prospect is not saying no; they are saying *"not
in this format, not right now, prove it asynchronously."*

**The website is where that proof lands.** Which means the homepage has a fourth audience Step 2 had
not modelled: **someone who has already spoken to Skyler, is mildly interested, and is now checking
whether the call was worth taking.**

That visitor is unlike the other three:

| Visitor | Arrives | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Referred | Warm, pre-sold on the person | Not to be disqualified |
| Search | Cold, comparing | Relevance, then differentiation |
| **Post-call ("send me info")** | **Lukewarm, mid-conversation, slightly sceptical of having been sold to** | **Confirmation that the caller was credible and that this is not a waste of time** |
| Eager (item 9) | Hot, decided | **A fast path to contact** |

🔴 **Practical asymmetry worth noting:** *"send me some information"* means **you send, I receive**.
The Free Website Audit as currently conceived asks the prospect to **submit** something first
([`AUDIT_RESEARCH.md`](AUDIT_RESEARCH.md)). Those are opposite directions of travel. ⚠️ A sendable
artefact and a requestable one may not be the same object — flagged for Skyler, not resolved here.

#### B6. Not every good client wants maximal growth
**Verdict: 🟢 CONFIRMED, and it contradicts the entire category's framing.**

Item 4 — *"consistent / content"* — is the clearest statement of this. Independently corroborated by
trade economics: an HVAC maintenance-plan customer is worth **$47,200** against **$1,200–3,500** for a
service-only customer (C3). **Customer quality outperforms customer quantity by more than 10× in this
buyer's own economics.** A contractor at capacity who wants *better* jobs is behaving rationally, not
timidly.

🔴 **Every competitor sampled sells growth.** *"grow revenue"*, *"dominate"*, *"attract more
customers"* (E1); *"More Calls from Google Maps"* (E2); *"Technology That Helps Small Businesses
Grow"* (E4). **All four are pitched at a goal a meaningful share of good-fit prospects do not have.**

Legitimate non-growth goals this buyer may hold: **credibility** · **better-quality opportunities** ·
**professionalism** · **protecting existing referral flow** · **reducing time spent chasing bad-fit
enquiries**.

⚠️ **This is the largest genuinely unclaimed position found anywhere in Step 2** — and it is
*adjacent* to V1's "close the gap" instinct without being the same thing. Recorded as a finding.
🚫 Not a concept and not a positioning decision; Step 3 owns that.

#### B7. The eager buyer must not be force-marched through education
**Verdict: 🟢 CONFIRMED, and almost certainly under-weighted.**

Item 9 is the only positive entry in a list built from rejections — and **a rejection-derived list
systematically under-records it**, because eager prospects end calls quickly and pleasantly, generating
less memory per contact ([`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md) F1).

🔴 **Corroborated hard by the buyer's own industry:** **78% of customers buy from the first business to
respond**; **85% of homeowners choose the first contractor who responds** (C6, C1). **This buyer's own
market punishes friction ruthlessly.** They will apply the same standard to Kreated.

⚠️ **Direct consequence for the persuasion sequence.** A sequence optimised for the sceptic taxes the
eager buyer, and the eager buyer is the most valuable visitor on the site. The sequence needs a
**bypass, available at every stage** — not a shorter sequence.

## 1. 🟢 First-party verbatim — the only fully trustworthy language here

From Missy Boyd's Google review ([`../docs/PROOF.md`](../docs/PROOF.md) §5, quoted exactly there).
Phrases a **satisfied** buyer of this exact service actually used:

| Phrase | What it reveals |
|---|---|
| *"helped me make my idea come to life"* | The buyer frames the purchase as **their idea being realised**, not as a service delivered to them |
| *"asked my input at every turn"* | **Collaboration** is named before quality |
| *"deciding on how I wanted things worded, color schemes and pictures"* | The buyer's mental model of the work is **words, colour, pictures** — not strategy, architecture or search |
| *"changed things as soon as I asked"* | **Responsiveness during the work**, not just before the sale |
| *"never letting more than a couple of hours pass without a response, any day of the week"* | Response speed stated in **hours and days of the week** |
| *"only took two weeks to fully develop my site and have it up and running"* | **Duration is a satisfaction driver**, volunteered unprompted |
| *"my new website and branding/logo"* | Website and brand are **one purchase** in the buyer's mind |
| *"I would recommend him over and over again"* | The referral loop closing — *him*, a person, not a company |

🔴 **The single most important observation in this file:** this buyer's vocabulary contains **no**
marketing terminology whatsoever. No SEO, no conversion, no visibility, no funnel, no brand
strategy, no ROI. The words are *idea, wording, colors, pictures, response, two weeks, recommend*.

⚠️ Compare with V1's designated load-bearing words —
`system` · `evidence` · `visibility` · `conversion` · `foundations` · `scoped` · `documented`
([`../docs/BRAND.md`](../docs/BRAND.md) §C). **A real satisfied client used none of them.** Recorded
as a contradiction in [`STEP_2_SYNTHESIS.md`](STEP_2_SYNTHESIS.md) §15.

## 3. 🔴 Inferred vocabulary, by theme

Derived from behavioural evidence. **Hypotheses about language, not recorded speech.**

⚠️ **Scored against F1 (2026-08-19).** Now that real Kreated prospect language exists, the inferences
below can be checked:

| Inferred theme | F1 verdict |
|---|---|
| Marketing scepticism — *"what am I actually paying for?"* | ⚠️ **Not corroborated.** Nothing in F1 expresses scepticism about agencies. The conversation dies before scepticism is reachable |
| Fear of wasting money — *"what's this going to run me?"* | 🔴 **Contradicted at first contact.** Cost appears **nowhere** in F1 |
| Website frustration — *"it just sits there"* | 🔴 **Contradicted.** F1 says the opposite: *"We already have a website"* — no frustration expressed |
| Lead frustration — *"the phone's been quiet"* | ⚠️ **Not corroborated.** F1 says *"we're consistent / content"* |
| Desire for growth — *"I want bigger jobs, not more jobs"* | 🟢 **Corroborated in direction.** F1 item 4 confirms many do not want growth at all |
| Embarrassment — *"we look smaller than we are"* | ⚠️ **Not corroborated.** Never volunteered |

🔴 **The pattern is stark and worth stating plainly: almost every inferred frustration failed to
appear.** Two readings, both plausible, and they are not mutually exclusive:

1. **The frustrated buyer is not who Kreated has been calling.** Cold outreach reaches the contented
   majority; the frustrated minority is who *arrives* — by referral or search — already looking.
2. **Frustration is not admitted to a stranger on the phone.** Nobody tells an unsolicited caller that
   their website embarrasses them.

⚠️ **Under either reading, the same conclusion follows:** the inferred vocabulary below may describe
the **website visitor** accurately while describing the **cold-call prospect** not at all. They are
different populations. 🚫 Do not treat F1 as having falsified this section — treat it as having
revealed that Step 2 was modelling two audiences as one.

### Website frustration
- "it just sits there" · "nobody ever calls from it"
- "it's fine, it's just old"
- "my nephew built it" / "I built it myself on Wix"
- "it looks terrible on a phone"
- "I've been meaning to redo it for two years"
- ⚠️ *Basis:* 64% default to builders over custom dev (B1); mobile bounce 12.1 points above desktop
  (B5); average mobile load 8.6s (B4).

### Lead frustration
- "the phone's been quiet"
- "we're busy in summer and dead in January"
- "I don't know where my jobs come from"
- "we used to get more calls"
- ⚠️ *Basis:* deals are lost in private research the owner never sees (C1); 73% not confident their
  marketing works (B8). **The owner has the symptom without the cause** — this vocabulary is
  therefore vague by nature, and that vagueness is itself the finding.

### Marketing scepticism
- "everybody says they'll get me to number one"
- "I get five of those calls a week"
- "what am I actually paying for?"
- "they sent me a report I didn't understand"
- "I couldn't tell you what they did"
- ⚠️ *Basis:* "black box" reports (C10); 38% switch annually over inability to see what they pay for
  (C10); "reports full of impressions, clicks, and traffic increases that did not correlate with
  revenue" (C10).

### Embarrassment / credibility
- "I don't send people to my website"
- "I'd rather just tell them to call me"
- "we look smaller than we are"
- "it doesn't look like what we actually do"
- ⚠️ *Basis:* 50ms credibility judgment (A3); aesthetic treatment raises judged credibility of
  identical content (A5). 🔴 **The mechanism is well evidenced; this specific phrasing is not.**
  Closest thing in the research to V1's "close the gap" — and it remains **inferred**.

### Desire for growth
- "I want to stay booked through the winter"
- "I want bigger jobs, not more jobs"
- "I want to stop chasing"
- ⚠️ *Basis:* trade economics (C3, C4) — a maintenance-plan HVAC customer is worth $47,200 vs
  $1,200–3,500 for service-only. **Job quality beats job quantity in this buyer's economics**, so
  language about "more leads" may actively mis-target them.

### Fear of wasting money
- "what's this going to run me?"
- "I don't want to get into something monthly"
- "last time I spent X and got nothing"
- "is this a contract?"
- ⚠️ *Basis:* the 60× local price spread (E2, E3, C9); the freeze (C8); universal competitor
  pre-emption of contract fear (E1, E3).
