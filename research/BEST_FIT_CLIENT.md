## The qualifying arithmetic

Everything below derives from one calculation, per Skyler's brief that *"one additional
customer/job can have meaningful value."*

**The filter is not industry. It is: (job value × close rate) against a revenue base able to fund a
four-figure project without pain.**

Two hard numbers set the floor:
- **66.3% of small businesses spend under $1,000/year on marketing** (C7). Most of the market cannot
  buy at any agency price.
- **SBA benchmark is 7–8% of gross revenue**; SMB actuals run **5–10%** (C7).

At 7%, a **$500k** business has ~$35k/yr total marketing capacity. A **$150k** business has ~$10k —
where a website consumes most of a year's budget and the purchase becomes painful regardless of
value.

Against typical project pricing — **$2,000–$8,000**, $15,000+ advanced (C9); Raleigh-local
**$1,500–$5,000+** (E2) and **$500–$7,000+** (E3) — the practical revenue floor is roughly
**$400k–$500k**.

⚠️ **That floor is derived, not measured.** Kreated has never recorded a client's revenue
([`BUYER_PROFILES.md`](BUYER_PROFILES.md) §5). Treat it as a working threshold to be checked against
Skyler's actual client history, not as a validated number.

## 🟢 IDEAL FIT

**Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH.** Two of Kreated's three real clients match.

### Profile
An **established, owner-operated local service business** where a single job is worth **$3,000+**,
revenue is roughly **$500k–$5M**, and the owner is the decision-maker.

| Signal | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **One job pays for the project** | Roofing margins 34–42% with only 5–15% repeat over 10 years — every job won cold (C4). HVAC new install CLV **$8,000–15,000**; maintenance-plan customer **$47,200** (C3) |
| **Already gets referrals but is invisible in search** | Referrals close at **40–70%** at $0–52/lead (C5) — proof of demand exists. Kreated adds a channel rather than creating one |
| **Has a website that undersells them** | 50ms credibility judgment (A3); **53% of mobile visitors leave after 3 seconds** (B4); average mobile load **8.6s** (B4) |
| **Their customers search before contact** | **98% of consumers search online to find local businesses**, up from 90% in 2019 (C7); **88% of near-me searches are mobile** (C7) |
| **Owner answers the phone** | 🔴 **Non-negotiable.** **95% of 466 home-services companies failed the 5-minute response test; 40% never responded** (C6); **78% buy from the first responder** (C6) |
| **A specific trigger has occurred** | Lost job, competitor comparison, referral that didn't convert ([`PAINS_AND_TRIGGERS.md`](PAINS_AND_TRIGGERS.md) Tier 1) |

### Sectors with evidence behind them
Restoration · roofing · HVAC · plumbing · electrical · pool and leak services · remodeling and
specialty trades. **Matches Rare Raleigh (restoration) and LLEC (pool leak detection) directly.**

### Why they are ideal for *Kreated specifically*
- The ROI conversation is short and arithmetic-driven — Stage 3 of the
  [`PERSUASION_SEQUENCE.md`](PERSUASION_SEQUENCE.md) does the work.
- Their problem is **diagnosable**, which is Kreated's strongest capability and the sequence's hinge.
- Their competitors are visibly winning on exactly the axis Kreated sells.
- The engagement is **project-shaped, not retainer-shaped** — matching a solo operator's capacity.

## 🟡 ACCEPTABLE FIT

**Confidence: MEDIUM.** Real, but weaker economics or weaker evidence.

### A. Credibility-constrained professional practices
Coaching, tutoring, therapy, consulting, specialty health, boutique legal/financial.

**Why acceptable:** Kreated's only complete case study, only testimonial, and only 4-service
engagement come from here (LearnSmart). Brand Strategy and Web Design attach naturally
([`OFFER_FIT.md`](OFFER_FIT.md) §6). Frequently no website at all — **~27% of small businesses have
none** (B6, B7).

**Why not ideal:** lower ticket, lower repeat, weaker one-job arithmetic. Growth capped by the
practitioner's own capacity — they can only see so many clients.

⚠️ **Tension with [`../docs/DECISIONS.md`](../docs/DECISIONS.md) 007:** the proof for this segment is
off the homepage, so the homepage will under-serve the segment where Kreated's evidence is
strongest.

### B. Newly serious operators
New ownership, rebrand, second location, first employee, revenue jump.

**Why acceptable:** best budget psychology of any segment — money is already allocated to "the
change", and the buyer is choosing *who*, not *whether*.

**Why not ideal:** ⚠️ **entirely unevidenced.** No research found; no Kreated engagement of this type.
Reasoned, not observed.

### C. Multi-location or franchise local operators
**Why acceptable:** higher budget, real complexity, GBP work scales across locations. A competitor
testimonial evidences demand: *"Keeping all 50 of our locations at the top of Google Search"* (E2).

**Why not ideal:** ⚠️ likely needs ongoing support and reporting cadence a solo operator cannot
honestly promise ([`OBJECTIONS.md`](OBJECTIONS.md) #9).

## 🔴 BAD FIT

Documented because attracting these costs real time and produces unhappy clients.

| Bad fit | Why | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **The sub-$1,000/year marketing majority** | Cannot buy at any agency price. **66.3% of small businesses** (C7). AI builders now serve them — and **93–95% of AI-builder users are first-time website owners** (B1), i.e. exactly this group | C7, B1 |
| **Businesses under ~$400k revenue** | At 7% of revenue the project is disproportionate and the relationship starts strained | C7, C9 |
| **Businesses whose customer is worth under ~$100** | The arithmetic never closes. No number of extra visits justifies the spend | C3, C4 |
| 🔴 **Businesses that won't answer the phone** | **The single most important disqualifier.** Kreated's work delivers leads into a void, the client sees no result, and attributes the failure to Kreated. **95% fail the 5-minute test** (C6) | C6, C1 |
| **Buyers wanting guaranteed rankings** | 🚫 Kreated cannot and must not promise this ([`../docs/PROOF.md`](../docs/PROOF.md) §6). ⚠️ Competitors promise adjacent things — "dominate Google" (E1) — so this buyer will find someone to tell them yes | Policy |
| **Pure price shoppers** | A $500 Google Site is available locally (E3). Kreated cannot win and should not try | E3 |
| **Ecommerce / product businesses** | Different problem, different stack, no delivered evidence | — |
| **Buyers needing a team, account management or 24/7 support** | 🚫 Founder-led solo cannot serve this honestly; claiming otherwise breaks [`../docs/PROOF.md`](../docs/PROOF.md) §6 | Policy |
| **Buyers needing paid media** | Kreated sells no Google Ads or LSAs; a balanced contractor lead mix is **~40% paid** (C5). Competitors bundle it (E1) | C5, E1 |
| **"Fix it by next week" buyers** | ⚠️ Two weeks is Kreated's *fast* case (LearnSmart), for a small practice site — not a promise for a trade operator's rebuild | Project fact |
| **Buyers who want to hand it over and never engage** | Kreated's only positive testimonial is built on collaboration — *"asked my input at every turn"*. A disengaged client removes the working method that produced the one good outcome on record | First-party |

## Signals to discourage — and the honest way to do it

⚠️ **Mechanism observations only. 🚫 Not copy, not layout, not design.**

The research supports discouragement through **specificity rather than exclusion**. Nobody in the
competitor sample says who they are wrong for ([`COMPETITOR_MESSAGING.md`](COMPETITOR_MESSAGING.md)
"What nobody says" #5) — all four are for everyone.

| Disqualifies | By what mechanism |
|---|---|
| Sub-floor budgets | Any concrete price anchor. ⚠️ Two competitors publish prices (E2, E3); Kreated publishes none, so this filter currently does not operate at all |
| Guarantee-seekers | Stating what will not be claimed. Kreated already does this better than anyone in the market |
| Team-expecting buyers | Founder-led framing, already required by [`../docs/PROOF.md`](../docs/PROOF.md) §6 |
| Non-responders | ⚠️ **The valuable one nobody does.** Saying plainly that a website cannot fix an unanswered phone qualifies buyers *and* demonstrates diagnostic honesty — Stage 2 of the persuasion sequence, doing double duty |
| Low-job-value businesses | Naming the arithmetic openly. A buyer whose jobs are worth $200 disqualifies themselves without being told |

🔴 **The most efficient qualification device found in the research is the arithmetic itself.** A page
that helps the buyer calculate what one additional job is worth attracts everyone for whom the number
works and dismisses everyone for whom it does not — **without Kreated making a single exclusionary
statement.**
