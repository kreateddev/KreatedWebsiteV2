# SOURCES

Step 2 research, conducted 2026-08-19. Every file in `research/` cites back to this list.

**Evidence grading used throughout Step 2:**

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A — Survey/panel** | Named methodology, stated sample size, representative panel |
| **B — Aggregated industry data** | Platform or vendor data at scale; methodology partly disclosed |
| **C — Vendor/agency claim** | Published by a party with a commercial interest in the number; no methodology |
| **D — Anecdotal** | Individual accounts, forum posts, single quotes, review-site complaints |
| **E — Primary observation** | Directly observed by Kreated during this research (e.g. reading a competitor's live site) |
| **F — First-party founder recall** | Skyler's recollection of real Kreated sales conversations. Not transcripts, not recordings, not counted. **Highest relevance, lowest precision** |

🚫 **Grade C is the most common grade in this industry and the most abused.** Marketing-statistics
roundups recycle each other; a number appearing on twenty sites is not twenty sources. Where a
figure below is widely repeated but traceable to no disclosed methodology, it is graded C regardless
of how often it appears.

## F — First-party founder recall

### F1 — Skyler's recalled cold-call and sales-conversation responses
**Added 2026-08-19 (Step 2 addendum). Kreated's only first-party buyer-side data.**

Responses Skyler recalls hearing repeatedly:

1. *"I'm not interested."*
2. *"I don't have time."*
3. *"We already have someone doing that."*
4. *"We're consistent / content with where we're at."*
5. *"Send me some information."*
6. *"Most of our business is word of mouth."*
7. *"We already have a website."*
8. *"We don't need a website."*
9. **Some prospects are immediately very eager to get started.**

### 🔴 How this source must and must not be used

**Must not:**
- 🚫 Not verbatim. Recalled paraphrase, not transcript.
- 🚫 Not statistical. No counts, no frequencies, no percentages exist. "Common" means *Skyler remembers
  hearing it*, which is subject to recall bias — memorable and repeated responses survive; unremarkable
  ones do not.
- 🚫 Not a representative sample. No record of how many calls, over what period, to which industries, at
  what revenue level, or what the conversion rate was.
- 🚫 Not to be quoted on the website as customer language.

**Must:**
- ✅ Treated as the **highest-relevance source in the entire research set**, because it is the only
  evidence drawn from Kreated's actual market rather than from the industry at large.
- ✅ Allowed to **outrank** contradicting secondary sources on questions of what Kreated's prospects
  actually say, while remaining subordinate on questions of population-level behaviour.

### 🔴 The interpretation hazard — read before using F1 anywhere

**These are cold-call responses. Cold-call responses are not homepage objections.**

The two differ in three ways that change their meaning:

| | Cold call | Website visit |
|---|---|---|
| Who is present | Everyone dialled, including bad fits | Self-selected visitors |
| Their state | Interrupted, mid-task, unprepared | Voluntarily looking |
| The response's job | **End the interruption** | Evaluate a decision |

Most of F1 items 1–3 are **ritual deflections**, whose social function is to close an unwanted call.
They carry very little information about what the prospect believes. Items 4, 6, 7 and 8 are
different — they are *reasons volunteered*, and reasons carry belief.

🔴 **Item 9 is the most under-weighted entry in the list.** A cold-call list naturally records
rejections, because rejections are what you have to get past. Eager prospects end the call quickly and
happily, so they generate less memory per contact — meaning **F1 almost certainly under-represents
them.**

⚠️ **Item 5 is the bridge.** *"Send me some information"* means the cold call does not end the sales
conversation — it **transfers it to whatever Kreated sends next.** That is the finding with the
largest structural consequence for the website, and it is developed in
[`PERSUASION_SEQUENCE.md`](PERSUASION_SEQUENCE.md) §"Entry contexts".

## 🔴 Method limitations — read before trusting any conclusion

1. **Reddit and forum data were not accessible.** Direct forum research was blocked at the crawler
   level. The plan called for mining owner language in the wild; that did not happen. **The
   voice-of-customer bank is therefore the weakest output of Step 2** and is labelled as such in
   [`VOICE_OF_CUSTOMER.md`](VOICE_OF_CUSTOMER.md).
2. **No first-party buyer research was conducted.** No interviews, no surveys of Kreated's own
   prospects, no win/loss analysis. Everything here is secondary except E1–E4.
3. **Zero behavioural data exists for Kreated.** V1 never installed analytics
   ([`../docs/CONVERSION.md`](../docs/CONVERSION.md) §5), so no funnel, bounce, source or search-query
   data about actual Kreated visitors exists anywhere.
4. **The competitor sample is four sites**, selected from one search of the Raleigh market. It is
   indicative, not representative. It skews toward agencies that rank for the term — i.e. toward
   agencies good at SEO, which is a biased sample for judging what typical competitors sound like.
5. **Much of the local-service data describes the buyer's *customer*, not the buyer.** That is
   deliberate and useful — it is what the buyer is trying to influence — but it must not be
   mistaken for evidence about how the buyer chooses an agency.
6. **Website-adoption figures conflict across sources** (73% vs 83% having a website). Both are
   cited; neither is treated as settled.
7. **2026 "statistics" pages are heavily recycled.** Where a striking number could not be traced to
   a methodology, it is graded C and flagged inline.
