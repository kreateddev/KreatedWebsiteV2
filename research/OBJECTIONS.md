## STAGE 1 OBJECTIONS — permission
**Evidenced by F1. 🚫 Recalled, not verbatim, not counted.**

### S1.1 "We're consistent / content with where we're at" — sufficiency
**Confidence: HIGH (F1 item 4; corroborated B7, B8, C3).**

The strongest belief in the F1 set and, on the evidence, **the true primary objection.**

Three things it may mean, indistinguishable from outside: genuinely at capacity and not seeking growth;
suspecting otherwise and unwilling to open the file; or a polite call-ender.

🔴 **All three punish being told they have a problem.** An unrequested diagnosis delivered to someone
who has just declared themselves content is received as an accusation.

⚠️ **It is also frequently correct.** An HVAC maintenance-plan customer is worth **$47,200** vs
**$1,200–3,500** for service-only (C3). A contractor at capacity who wants *better* jobs rather than
*more* is behaving rationally. **Treating contentment as an error to be corrected mis-reads a
legitimate business position** — and every competitor sampled does exactly that by selling growth
(E1, E2, E4).

### S1.2 "I don't have time" — attention cost
**Confidence: HIGH (F1 item 2; corroborated B8, A6).**

**56% of SMBs have ≤1hr/day for marketing**; **75% find agency selection time-consuming, 77% say it is
far from simple** (B8, A6).

🔴 **The under-examined half is the *ongoing* burden, not the purchase.** The question is not only
"what does this cost in money" but "what does this cost in **attention, permanently**" — decisions,
content, reviews, updates, someone to chase.

⚠️ **A real and unresolved tension:** Kreated's only positive testimonial is built on collaboration —
*"asked my input at every turn"* — which is precisely the burden this objection refuses. **Kreated's
best working method is this buyer's stated objection.** Not resolvable in research; flagged.

### S1.3 "We already have someone doing that" — incumbency
**Confidence: MEDIUM (F1 item 3).**

May be a real agency, a freelancer, a nephew, a spouse, or nobody. Cannot be distinguished on a call.

⚠️ Where real, switching costs apply and the relevant data is that **>60% of brands are unhappy with
their agency within the first year** and **38% switch SEO providers annually** (C10) — so incumbency is
weaker than it sounds, but the *moment* of weakness is not when Kreated happens to call.

### S1.4 "We already have a website" / "We don't need a website" — binary possession
**Confidence: HIGH (F1 items 7, 8; corroborated B1, B6, B7).**

🔴 **The largest category-level misunderstanding found in Step 2.** These responses are coherent only
if *website* is a thing you **have or lack**, not a thing whose **quality varies and affects results**.

Corroborated: **83% of small businesses now have a website** (B6); **64% default to builders over
custom development** (B1); **34% of those without one say it is "not relevant to my industry"** (B7).

**The market has moved from "do you have one" to "is yours losing." This buyer's mental model has
not.**

⚠️ **Direct consequence:** Website Redesign — the best-fitting service for the primary buyer
([`OFFER_FIT.md`](OFFER_FIT.md) §6) — **is not a category this buyer knows they are in.** They believe
they already solved this problem.

⚠️ 🚫 **This is not licence to tell them their website is bad.** S1.1 guarantees that lands as an
attack. The only non-hostile crossing from *possession* to *performance* runs through **evidence about
their own results**, not judgement about their site.

### S1.5 "I'm not interested" — ritual close
**Confidence: HIGH that it is said; 🔴 near-zero information value (F1 item 1).**

Recorded for completeness. Carries no belief and supports no conclusion. 🚫 Do not design anything
around it.

## STAGE 2 OBJECTIONS — evaluation

⚠️ These were the original contents of this file. **They remain valid** — they are simply reached
later than Step 2 originally assumed, and are evidenced by secondary research rather than by F1.

## HIGH CONFIDENCE

### 1. "I don't know what this costs, and I suspect I can't afford it"
**Evidence: HIGH (C8, C9, E2, E3).**

The best-described objection in the research, and it is not simple price resistance — it is
**price-range vertigo**.

Published bands the buyer encounters:

| Source | Range |
|---|---|
| Agency websites generally (C9) | **$3,000–$30,000** |
| Typical small-business site 2026 (C9) | **$2,000–$8,000**, advanced $15,000+ |
| Tyndall, Raleigh (E2) | **$1,500–$5,000+** |
| Peak City, Raleigh (E3) | **$500 Google Sites → $7,000+ custom**; GBP optimization **$300** |

A **60× spread** between the cheapest and dearest local option for something described in nearly
identical words. The buyer cannot tell whether they are shopping for a $500 thing or a $30,000
thing — so they postpone.

The mechanism is documented precisely: owners "know they need something better than a Facebook page
but also know that an agency quoting $50,000 is solving a different problem than the one they have,
causing most owners to freeze" (C8).

🔴 **This is a freeze, not a rejection.** The buyer is not choosing a competitor. They are choosing
nothing — which makes "do nothing" the primary competitive alternative
([`COMPETITIVE_ALTERNATIVES.md`](COMPETITIVE_ALTERNATIVES.md) §1).

⚠️ **Two of four Raleigh competitors publish prices** (E2, E3). Kreated publishes none
([`../docs/BUSINESS.md`](../docs/BUSINESS.md) §4 — pricing appears nowhere in V1). **Not a
recommendation to publish pricing** — that is Skyler's commercial decision — but the buyer is
resolving this objection on competitors' sites and cannot resolve it on Kreated's.

### 2. "I've paid for this before and got nothing"
**Evidence: HIGH (C10, D2, and the >60% figure).**

**>60% of brands report being unhappy with their agency within the first year** (C10). **38% of
businesses switch SEO providers annually**, primarily citing "lack of transparency" and "inability
to see what we're paying for" (C10). Owners report receiving "reports full of impressions, clicks,
and traffic increases that did not correlate with revenue growth or improved lead quality" (C10).

Anecdotal but consistent (D2): *"a completely horrible experience, a huge waste of time and money"*
· *"a total waste of time, energy, and money"* — the second attached to a **$2,409** upfront payment.

The industry description is blunt: vague retainers, hidden fees, rigid contracts, and **"black box"
reports** leaving owners unable to judge ROI (C10).

🔴 **This objection is pre-loaded before Kreated says anything.** The buyer is not sceptical of
Kreated. They are sceptical of the category, and Kreated inherits it.

### 3. "I can't tell if SEO is real"
**Evidence: HIGH (C10).**

A distinct objection from #2 and worth separating: the *deliverable itself* is unobservable. A
website is a visible artefact; ranking work is not. Clients "never see the methodology behind
decisions, so when they leave, they can't replicate what worked because they owned the output but
not the system that produced it" (C10).

⚠️ **Direct consequence for Kreated:** Local SEO and Google Business Profile Optimization — two of
five services — sit in the least-trusted, most-commoditised category in this market. Web Design and
Website Redesign produce a thing the buyer can see. See [`OFFER_FIT.md`](OFFER_FIT.md).

### 4. "I already get referrals — why would I pay for this?"
**Evidence: HIGH (C5, F1 item 6, and the same data cuts the other way).**

🔴 **PROMOTED 2026-08-19.** F1 confirms *"Most of our business is word of mouth"* as one of only four
beliefs Kreated's prospects volunteer unprompted. **This is not a Stage 2 objection at all — it
belongs in Stage 1**, and it is the most-stated reason in Kreated's real market. It stays documented
here because the counter-argument is developed below, but its rank has changed.

This is the *rational* objection, and the research validates it. Referrals: **$0–52 per lead, 40–70%
close rate** (C5) — genuinely superior economics to any paid channel. **92% of consumers trust
recommendations from friends and family more than any advertising** (C5).

The honest counter is not "referrals aren't enough." It is that **referrals are checked**: the
referred prospect still reads reviews (97%, A1) and still looks at the site (C1). Referrals are not
a substitute for a credible presence — **they are the traffic most damaged by an incredible one.**

⚠️ The counter-argument is sound but the causal chain is **inferred**, not measured. See
[`PAINS_AND_TRIGGERS.md`](PAINS_AND_TRIGGERS.md) #2.

### 5. "I don't have time for this"
**Evidence: HIGH (B8, C7).**

**56% of SMBs have an hour or less per day for marketing**; **47% of owners handle all their own
social media**; **73% are not confident their marketing is working** (B8, C7). **75% of clients say
the agency selection process is time-consuming; 77% say it is far from simple** (A6).

Not an excuse — a measured constraint. Any process requiring sustained buyer attention loses to
inertia. **This is the strongest argument in the entire research for a genuinely low-friction first
step.**

## MEDIUM CONFIDENCE

### 6. "My current site is good enough"
**Evidence: MEDIUM.**

Indirectly supported: **34% of businesses without a website say it is "not relevant to my industry"**
(B7) — evidence of a durable belief that online presence is optional in trades. Owners who feel this
about having *no* site plainly feel it about having a *dated* one.

⚠️ "Good enough" is usually a **proxy for #1 (cost) or #5 (time)**, not a genuine assessment.
Treat with suspicion; do not argue with it directly.

### 7. "Will this project ever actually finish?"
**Evidence: MEDIUM (C8, C12).**

Big-agency experience is described as "big promises in the sales call, a templated process dressed
up as custom work, and radio silence after launch" (C8). Against freelancers the fear inverts —
no backup, no continuity, "no way to supervise them and track their progress" (C12).

🔴 **Kreated sits in the worst position on this objection.** It is a solo operation, so it carries
the *freelancer* version of the fear (bus factor, disappearance) while charging agency-adjacent
prices. Two mitigations exist in Kreated's own material and neither is currently used as a trust
asset: the LearnSmart engagement finished in **two weeks** start to finish (client's own words,
[`../docs/PROOF.md`](../docs/PROOF.md) §5), and Rare Raleigh's incomplete state is **openly
labelled** rather than hidden.

### 8. "Will I be locked into a retainer?"
**Evidence: MEDIUM (E1, E2, E3, C10).**

Inferred largely from how loudly competitors pre-empt it — a reliable signal that they hear it
constantly:

- Perry SEO: *"No contracts. Cancel anytime."* · *"Month-to-month. We earn your business every month
  with results, not lock-in clauses."* (E1)
- Peak City: *"No Lock-In Contracts"* · *"You own your website and all your SEO work"* · *"We don't
  hold anything hostage"* (E3)

Underlying industry reality: "vague retainers, hidden fees, rigid contracts" (C10).

⚠️ **Because every competitor pre-empts it, saying "no lock-in" is table stakes, not
differentiation.** See [`COMPETITOR_MESSAGING.md`](COMPETITOR_MESSAGING.md).

### 9. "Are they big enough / will they still be here?"
**Evidence: MEDIUM (A6, C12).**

"How long an agency has been in business and how long they retain clients remain strong signals of
reliability, with case studies, client tenure, and consistent reporting doing more to build
confidence than flashy pitches" (A6). Agencies are chosen for backup: "if your main developer has an
emergency, someone else steps in" (C12).

🔴 **Kreated is structurally exposed here** — young, solo, three documented engagements, one review.
Competitors counter with tenure: **"15+ Years Experience"**, **"700+ Websites Built"** (E3),
**"helped over 150 clients"** (E2), **"87%+ Retention Rate"** (E1).

⚠️ But note the countervailing evidence in [`BUYING_CRITERIA.md`](BUYING_CRITERIA.md): "honesty,
professionalism, and reliability from the people a business will actually work with continue to
outweigh flash or even raw experience" (A6). Size is a **reassurance**, not a **criterion** — which
is a meaningful distinction and probably Kreated's escape route.

### 10. "I'll be sold things I don't need"
**Evidence: MEDIUM (C10).**

Supported by the transparency literature and by how insistently competitors deny it — "No wasted
spend" (E1), "we'll tell you honestly whether to optimize or start fresh" (E3), "not cookie-cutter
SEO packages" (E3).

⚠️ **Sharpened by Kreated's own structure.** Kreated sells five connected services and argues they
are one decision ([`../docs/SERVICES.md`](../docs/SERVICES.md) §3). To a buyer primed for upselling,
"these are all connected" and "you need all five" are hard to distinguish. **The connection argument
is a genuine differentiator that reads as an upsell if introduced too early.** See
[`PERSUASION_SEQUENCE.md`](PERSUASION_SEQUENCE.md).

## The objection hierarchy — REVISED 2026-08-19

Ordered by what must be neutralised first. **Stage 1 objections now outrank every Stage 2 objection**,
because a prospect who never grants permission never reaches evaluation.

| Rank | Objection | Stage | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Sufficiency** — *"we're consistent / content"* | 1 | 🟢 **F1 first-party** |
| **2** | **Referral sufficiency** — *"most of our business is word of mouth"* | 1 | 🟢 **F1 first-party** + C5 |
| **3** | **Binary possession** — *"we already have a website"* | 1 | 🟢 **F1 first-party** + B1, B6 |
| **4** | **Time and ongoing attention cost** | 1 | 🟢 **F1** + B8, A6 |
| **5** | **Incumbency** — *"someone already does that"* | 1 | 🟡 F1 |
| 6 | Cost vertigo — the 60× spread and the freeze | 2 | Secondary (C8, C9, E2, E3) |
| 7 | Category scepticism — burned before, SEO unverifiable | 2 | Secondary (C10, D2) |
| 8 | Completion and continuity | 2 | Secondary (C8, C12) — 🔴 Kreated's structural weak point |
| 9 | Entrapment / retainers | 2 | Secondary (E1, E3) — table stakes |

### What changed and why

- **Cost fell from 1 to 6.** It is real and well evidenced — but it is evidenced about buyers who are
  *already evaluating*. Kreated's prospects do not get there.
- **Referral sufficiency rose from 4 to 2.** Volunteered unprompted in real conversations.
- **Sufficiency is new at #1.** It did not exist in the original hierarchy because no secondary source
  captures it — surveys ask people who are shopping, and this belief is the reason people are not.
- **Binary possession is new at #3.** Also invisible to secondary research.

🔴 **The strategic consequence.** Step 2 originally concluded that the primary competitor is *doing
nothing* and that only **diagnosis** beats inertia. F1 sharpens that: **the buyer's inertia is not
passive ignorance — it is an actively held position** (*consistent, content, word of mouth, already
have a website*). Diagnosis still beats it, but only if it arrives as **information the buyer can
check**, never as a **correction of their stated position**.

⚠️ **And the two Kreated is worst-positioned on structurally — completion (#8) and continuity — are
now ranked lower, not because they matter less, but because they are reached less often.** They remain
the reason a Stage 2 prospect declines.

🔴 **The two Kreated is worst-positioned on — #7 completion and #9 continuity — are exactly the two
that solo operation creates.** They cannot be argued away. They can only be answered with evidence
of finished work, which is why [`../docs/DECISIONS.md`](../docs/DECISIONS.md) 007 and 008 have real
persuasion cost: of the two homepage Work candidates, one is unfinished by definition and the other
has no case-study narrative. Documented for Skyler in
[`STEP_2_SYNTHESIS.md`](STEP_2_SYNTHESIS.md) §15.
